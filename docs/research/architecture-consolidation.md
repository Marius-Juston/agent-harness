## 0. Findings in one paragraph
Build the component as **three parts, not one agent**: (1) a deterministic trigger in devkit, shown by `uv run dev orient`, that fires when code doubled and grew by 5,000 LOC, after 100 first-parent commits, or when a new structural smell appears (an orphaned dormant subtree, a large churny file, a lazy-import cycle, a high share of function-level imports); replayed over the four sibling repositories it fired 11 times in the large repository's 623 commits, 4 times in a mid-sized one's 326 and never in the two lean ones, and it costs 0.4 s on 259k LOC; (2) a `dev` report command that gathers the slower measurements in seconds; (3) a read-only `harness-ops:architect` subagent that turns the report into a feature inventory and a proposal filed as small, separate issues, so it never edits or deletes anything itself. The owner's "100k could be 20k" is not supported by the data: on the largest repository the behaviour-preserving cut is about 12%, and every owner decision together reaches about 21.5%; going past 25-30% needs scope or architecture decisions that no measurement here can show to be safe. Experts agree on the method: keep complexity down continuously, restructure incrementally behind seams with the system green at every step, and never rewrite from scratch. LOC is a report, not the goal. "Never delete progress" becomes a protocol: inventory first, owner approval for every feature cut, refactor-only PRs, a test baseline that may not shrink, and a restore point for every removal.

## 1. Question and scope
When and how should an agent-run repository step back and re-architect itself, keeping every feature and its robustness at a fraction of the code, without deleting progress? The note covers triggers, techniques, safe-refactor protocols, evidence on AI-generated code growth, reusable tools, and measurements of four sibling repositories, which appear under code names: **repo-L** (a game server, the large one), **repo-R** (a robotics simulation), **repo-E** (a research-first project that consumes this harness) and **H** (this repository). Out of scope: language style rules. The decision it serves is the shape and trigger of #113.

## 2. What experts prescribe
- **Minimise complexity, not lines.** SOURCED: Ousterhout defines complexity as what makes a system hard to change (change amplification, cognitive load, unknown unknowns), says it accrues incrementally, and asks for deep modules over many shallow ones ([CS190 notes](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/cs190-winter18/lecture.php?topic=complexity), [modular design](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/cs190-winter18/lecture.php?topic=modularDesign)); against over-decomposition he says "If two pieces of code are tightly related, the solution is to bring them together" ([aposd-vs-clean-code](https://github.com/johnousterhout/aposd-vs-clean-code)).
- **LOC can rise when design improves.** SOURCED: at Microsoft the top 5% most-refactored Windows 7 modules lost more dependencies and complexity than the rest but "increase size more" ([doi:10.1109/TSE.2014.2318734](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2014.2318734), abstract); the FSE 2012 version reports fewer post-release defects for a centralized, multi-year refactoring effort ([doi:10.1145/2393596.2393655](https://doi.org/10.1145/2393596.2393655), abstract). BELIEVED: a consolidation gate on LOC would reward the wrong thing.
- **Boundaries hide decisions likely to change.** SOURCED: Parnas, "it is almost always incorrect to begin the decomposition of a system into modules on the basis of a flowchart"; each module hides a design decision ([Parnas 1972 PDF](https://wstomv.win.tue.nl/edu/2ip30/references/criteria_for_modularization.pdf)). Foote and Yoder: keep together what changes at similar rates ([Big Ball of Mud](http://www.laputan.org/mud/mud.html)). BELIEVED: a consolidation groups code by co-change and by the decision it hides, not by pipeline step or by the issue that created the file.
- **Growth is certain; rising complexity is common but contested.** SOURCED: Lehman's law of continuing growth holds in every study a systematic review covers, while increasing complexity and declining familiarity "have been invalidated for most of the cases", especially in libre software ([Herraiz et al. 2013 PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/2013-herraiz.pdf)). BELIEVED: justify a review by measured symptoms in the repository, not by "complexity always rises", and expect a growth trigger to fire repeatedly, so it must be cheap.
- **Tidy continuously; plan restructurings rarely.** SOURCED: Fowler calls refactoring "an opportunistic activity" and says a team that does it well "should hardly ever need to plan refactoring" ([OpportunisticRefactoring](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/OpportunisticRefactoring.html)); Beck weighs each tidying by cost, coupling and cohesion ([Beck 2022](https://newsletter.kentbeck.com/p/conclusion)) and keeps structure and behaviour changes in separate PRs (book not fetched; [Warne review](https://henrikwarne.com/2024/01/10/tidy-first/)). Ousterhout's hedged budget for strategic work is "10-20% overhead?" ([CS190, working code](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/cs190-winter18/lecture.php?topic=working)). BELIEVED: an agent merging one issue at a time is tactical by construction, so a periodic, budgeted strategic pass is a reasonable addition, split into many small PRs.
- **Restructure incrementally, behind seams, green at every step.** SOURCED: strangler fig, because big-bang replacement goes "down in flames most of the time" ([Fowler 2024](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/StranglerFigApplication.html)); branch by abstraction, where "the system builds and runs correctly at all times" ([Fowler 2014](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/BranchByAbstraction.html)); parallel change (expand, migrate, contract), where skipping the contract phase can leave you "in a worse state than you started" ([Sato 2014](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ParallelChange.html)); the Mikado method for changes that keep breaking something else ([Methods & Tools](https://www.methodsandtools.com/archive/mikado.php)).
- **Feature parity is a trap.** SOURCED: promising "exactly what they had today" led to a big-bang cut-over plan, and pinning down current behaviour was itself a large effort ([Patterns of Legacy Displacement](https://martinfowler.com/articles/patterns-legacy-displacement/)); much legacy behaviour "isn't really wanted" ([Fowler 2024](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/StranglerFigApplication.html)). BELIEVED: the inventory sorts each behaviour into keep, drop with owner approval, or unused; it does not freeze everything.
- **Never rewrite from scratch.** SOURCED: Spolsky calls it "the single worst strategic mistake", because old code holds bug fixes ([Spolsky 2000](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2000/04/06/things-you-should-never-do-part-i/)); Foote and Yoder allow reconstruction only for code "beyond repair, or even comprehension", and first suggest cordoning it off ([Big Ball of Mud](http://www.laputan.org/mud/mud.html)); sacrificial architecture replaces individual modules ([Fowler 2014](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/SacrificialArchitecture.html)). No peer-reviewed failure rate for big-bang rewrites was found; this is practitioner evidence.
- **Enforce boundaries mechanically afterwards.** SOURCED: Shopify's modular monolith (2.8M LOC, 37 components) blocks dependency and privacy violations in PRs with a static check that takes "a few minutes", and moved to "more guidance and fewer rules" when hard rules added friction ([Under Deconstruction](https://shopify.engineering/shopify-monolith)). The Python counterpart is import-linter (§7).
- **Record why code was removed.** SOURCED: an ADR that is superseded rather than edited avoids "blind acceptance or blind reversal" ([Nygard 2011](https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)). BELIEVED: this matters more for agents, which otherwise re-add a removed layer.

Detail and the full "situation, technique" table: [r1-expert-guidance.md](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/r1-expert-guidance.md).

## 3. Evidence on agent-written code
- **Agents stop reusing code as a session goes on.** SOURCED: in RepoReuse (GPT-5.6 Terra, DeepSeek-v4.1-flash, Qwen3.7-plus, GLM-5.3; 2026), task chains with a cross-turn re-implementation rose from 13.8% at turn 1 to 50.8% at turn 5; pushing the signatures and behaviour of earlier functions ("interface memory") cut redundancy from 65.1% to 46.7% and lines written by 34% with the pass rate flat, while pasting full source did not help ([arXiv:2609.35357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35357)). One benchmark and one memory ablation, and the map is confounded with an explicit reuse request. The 34% is VERIFIED arithmetic ([r1_numbers.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/r1_numbers.py)).
- **Velocity spike, lasting complexity.** SOURCED: in 806 Cursor-adopting repositories against 1,380 matched controls, lines added rose 281% in month 1 and the gain vanished after month 2, while static-analysis warnings rose 30% and cognitive complexity 42%, persistently; the model was not recorded ([arXiv:2511.04427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04427)). The authors' 3x and 5x break-even figures could not be re-derived from the quoted coefficients; a naive reading gives 1.5x and 1.8x ([r1_numbers.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/r1_numbers.py)).
- **Debt survives, but agents also fix smells.** SOURCED: 302.6k AI-authored commits introduced 484k issues, 22.7% of which survive at HEAD, and fixed a similar number of smells as they introduced; there is no human baseline ([arXiv:2603.28592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28592)).
- **Contrary evidence.** SOURCED: an RCT with 151 participants found no significant maintainability penalty when other developers later evolved AI-assisted code, though it covers one feature in one Java application ([arXiv:2507.00788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00788)). Industry telemetry (copy/paste lines 8.3% to 12.3% from 2021 to 2024, moved lines from 25% to under 10%) does not say how it identifies AI code, so it carries little weight ([GitClear 2025](https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research)).
- **Agents refactor locally, not architecturally.** SOURCED: across 15,451 agent refactorings, the commonest are type changes and renames, with small structural gains ([arXiv:2511.04824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04824)); on 100 multi-file refactoring tasks an agent (SWE-agent with GPT-4o, 2025) solved 22% against 87% for a time-limited human, and a state representation helped by 43.9% relative ([arXiv:2503.07832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07832)). BELIEVED: agents will not consolidate architecture unprompted; it has to be triggered and scoped.
- **Refactorings ship silently broken at a few percent each.** SOURCED: differential fuzzing found 19-35% of LLM refactorings non-equivalent, about 21% of which passed the existing tests ([arXiv:2602.15761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15761)), so 4.0-7.4% ship silently broken (VERIFIED arithmetic, [refactor_risk.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/refactor_risk.py)); with GPT-5.1, iterative readability rewrites lost under 1% correctness per iteration ([arXiv:2602.21833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21833)). Older-model figures are upper bounds for current models.
- **Existing tests cover little of what a refactoring touches.** SOURCED: in three Java systems only 22% of refactored methods and fields were covered by existing regression tests, and refactorings were 8% of changes but involved in "almost half of the failed test cases" ([doi:10.1109/ICSM.2012.6405293](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSM.2012.6405293), abstract).
- **Risk compounds with the number of steps, not with batching.** VERIFIED ([refactor_risk.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/refactor_risk.py)): at 4% silent breakage per step, 10 steps give a 33.5% chance of at least one break and 50 steps 87%; at 1%, 50 steps give 39.5%. BELIEVED: small PRs do not lower the total risk; they make each break visible within one PR, revertible and bisectable, which is why Google shards large changes ([SWE at Google ch. 22](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch22.html)). Hence a stronger oracle than the suite (§5).
- **Keep consolidation out of feature PRs.** SOURCED: refactoring tangled into agent fixes is associated with reduced compilability across 3,691 patches and 12 LLMs ([arXiv:2605.22526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22526)).
- **Where cost concentrates.** SOURCED: relative churn predicts defect density far better than absolute churn ([doi:10.1145/1062455.1062514](https://doi.org/10.1145/1062455.1062514), abstract); the top 5 architectural debts cover 8-25% of error-prone files but 20-61% of maintenance effort in 7 projects ([doi:10.1145/2884781.2884822](https://doi.org/10.1145/2884781.2884822), abstract); defect-prediction models transfer poorly between projects ([doi:10.1145/1595696.1595713](https://doi.org/10.1145/1595696.1595713), abstract). BELIEVED: aim at hotspots, and calibrate triggers on each repository's own history.
- **Raw duplication is a weak signal.** SOURCED: 52% of clone groups were changed inconsistently, and unintentional inconsistencies were faulty about half the time ([arXiv:1701.05472](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.05472)), but in other systems "the great majority of bugs are not significantly associated with clones" ([Rahman et al. 2010](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/rahman2010cws.pdf)). BELIEVED: target new copies of existing code and co-changing clones, not a duplication percentage.

Detail: [r1-empirical.md](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/r1-empirical.md), [r1-against-and-safety.md](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/r1-against-and-safety.md).

## 5. A protocol that keeps progress
BELIEVED, assembled from §2-§4 and the sources named in each step. Continuous tidying stays the default; a periodic consolidation is for problems no local tidy can remove (two parallel subsystems, a wrong layer split, a dead subtree).
1. **Trigger, then a deterministic report.** The review starts from measurements, not from an agent's impression (§6 for the signals).
2. **Feature inventory before any proposal.** Sources, cheapest first: the CLI command tree and how often transcripts ran each command; entry points and import reach; pyscn's module communities, which found feature-shaped slices in repo-L in 3.5 s (VERIFIED, [r2-feature-inventory.md](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/r2-feature-inventory.md)); and, where the repository declares requirements with evidence tests, per-test coverage contexts ("software reconnaissance", [doi:10.1002/smr.4360070105](https://doi.org/10.1002/smr.4360070105), abstract), which cost one extra suite run and must treat import-time lines separately (a naive count reported 33.5% unused instead of 5.9%; VERIFIED). Each feature is classed keep, owner decision, or unused-candidate. Nothing is called unused when it could be reached dynamically (migrations, `importlib`, framework callbacks), a rule taken from Anthropic's code-modernization plugin ([plugin](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-plugins-official/tree/main/plugins/code-modernization)).
3. **The proposal separates three kinds of step,** each with an LOC estimate and the command that measured it: mechanical (behaviour-preserving deletions and merges), owner decisions (feature cuts, workflow choices) and redesign (per module, by strangler fig or parallel change). A feature cut needs the owner's explicit yes: "'no objection' is not approval" (code-modernization's brief step, [plugin](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-plugins-official/tree/main/plugins/code-modernization)).
4. **One issue per step, one refactor-only PR per issue**, executed by the normal builder flow, never inside a feature PR (§3).
5. **Each step proves it preserved behaviour:** characterisation tests for an untested surface before the move ([Feathers via InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2007/03/characterization-testing)); a test count that may not drop except for tests of an approved cut (the existing floor, [test-floors-and-coverage.md](test-floors-and-coverage.md)); a before/after comparison of test results plus a canary one-line break that must turn the suite red, the parts of code-modernization's `baseline_diff.py` and `proof_pack.py` worth reimplementing (Apache-2.0, [plugin](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-plugins-official/tree/main/plugins/code-modernization)); while old and new coexist, a property test `new(x) == old(x)`; mutation testing on the changed code where the suite is thin ([doi:10.1145/2635868.2635929](https://doi.org/10.1145/2635868.2635929), abstract); mechanical moves through a refactoring engine (rope, LibCST) rather than free-form rewriting ([arXiv:2411.04444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04444) re-applies LLM-chosen refactorings through a tested engine).
6. **A restore point for every removal.** The issue records what was removed, why, and the commit to restore it from; experiment results go into a research note before their scripts go; the review itself is recorded by a `Consolidation-Review:` trailer, which is also the trigger's reset point.
7. **Success is measured on structure:** smells cleared, cycles, churny files, and new-code duplication by one pinned tool. LOC is reported but never gated (§2).

## 6. Trigger calibration
VERIFIED by replaying every first-parent commit of the four repositories ([r2_simulate.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/r2_simulate.py), [r2_composite.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/r2_composite.py), over the output of `repo_metrics.py --replay`); counters reset at each fire, which models a review that removed nothing (the pessimistic case). Detail: [r2-trigger-calibration.md](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/r2-trigger-calibration.md).
- **Time and merged PRs are poor units at agent pace.** A 7-day clock fired 4 times in repo-L, all in dormant or slow stretches, and never in the others (repo-R added 35k LOC in 4 days). A 40-merged-PR counter first fired in repo-L at 135k LOC, because repo-L committed straight to the default branch for its first 63 commits. First-parent commits count PRs in a PR workflow and commits otherwise.
- **Doubling with a floor** ("code doubled and grew by at least 5k LOC") fires about log2(growth) times: 4 times over repo-L's 21x growth.
- **Orphaned dormancy caught the dead v1 tree 27 commits after it died**, with no false alarm in the other three: at least 5% of code in units that no commit touched in the last 30 first-parent commits and no active unit imports. Plain dormancy without the import filter flagged a live repo-R package. Known false negative: one documentation-only commit reset dormancy for 38 commits. Known false positive, found at synthesis: repo-L's `migrations/versions`, loaded dynamically, counts as orphaned (VERIFIED, `repo_metrics.py --trigger`).
- **Large churny files** (over 1,000 LOC, 30 or more non-merge commits) appeared early in repo-L and repo-R and never in H or repo-E; the first file-level signal inside `app/dev` came from this smell.
- **What no generic signal could see:** `app/dev` as a copy of a shared harness, because H did not exist until days before. The review must list the fastest-growing directories and compare command names with installed tooling.
- **Slow measures do not time accretion.** vulture's finding count tracks size (Pearson r 0.84-0.97 against LOC), pyscn's health grade stayed B throughout, and repo-L's pyscn duplication fell while it accreted. pyscn's result also depends on what is on disk (repo-R working tree 70 C and 10.7% duplication; tracked files only 88 B and 2.8%). These measures belong in the report, not the trigger.
- **The fast trigger** reads two trees through one `git cat-file --batch` and takes 0.41-0.46 s on repo-L and 0.08 s on H; over 114 sampled commits it never disagreed with the AST replay on whether a smell was present ([r2_agree.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/r2_agree.py)).
- **Prevention is cheaper.** A gate that runs pyscn clones and keeps only pairs where new code copies existing code fired on 7 of 109 repo-L merges, 6 of the 7 actionable, when restricted to type-1/2 clones outside class bodies, and on 0 of 28 H merges; recall is unmeasured ([backtest.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/backtest.py), [rules.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/rules.py); precision is one reviewer's judgement, BELIEVED). A signature map of H fits in about 10k tokens; repo-L's is about 205k, so it must be scoped to the packages a task touches ([mapsize.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/mapsize.py)). Agents in repo-L ran raw grep 16,450 times against 777 `dev code` calls ([navcount.py](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/scripts/navcount.py)). Detail: [r2-prevention.md](_scratch/architecture-consolidation/r2-prevention.md).

## 8. Component shape for #113
The owner suggested a subagent. The evidence supports a subagent for the judgement, but only fed by deterministic code.

| shape | fits | does not fit |
|---|---|---|
| `dev` command | the trigger and the report: deterministic, testable, gated and cheap (0.4 s; seconds with pyscn and vulture); [agent-harness.md](agent-harness.md) finds that enforcement beats instruction | the proposal: 9.2 of the 21.5 points in repo-L's plan need reading code and owner decisions (§4) |
| subagent | the proposal: its own context for a repository whose signature map alone is 205k tokens; read-only tools; a separate role, as in agentic refactoring where planner, executor and tester agents raised the test pass rate by 64.7% over a single agent ([doi:10.1145/3744916.3773153](https://doi.org/10.1145/3744916.3773153), abstract, Java, model-dated); the lead stays free to integrate | the trigger: an agent asked "is a review due?" has no history to compare against |
| skill | a few lines in the lead's routine on when to dispatch | running the review: a skill executes in the caller's context, which the lead must keep for integrating, and its text costs words in every session that loads it |
| separate plugin | nothing | `harness-ops` already ships agents, skills and hooks; a second plugin adds an install and update path, and pyscn's plugin shows the thin-wrapper failure mode |

Recommended (BELIEVED):
1. **A devkit trigger**, shown in `uv run dev orient` only when a review is due, with its reasons (for example "code 12k to 24k LOC; new smell: orphaned dormant `systems`, `core`"). Defaults from §6, kept in `[tool.devkit]`: growth factor 2.0 with a floor of 5,000 LOC; 100 first-parent commits; dormancy window 30 first-parent commits; orphaned share 5%; churny file 1,000 LOC and 30 commits; lazy-import cycle of 10 modules; indented-import share 10% when there are at least 50 imports; and an exclude glob for dynamically loaded trees such as migrations. Reset point: the newest commit with a `Consolidation-Review:` trailer, else the adoption commit. Days since review are shown for information only. Expected rate: about one review per 50-80 first-parent commits in an accreting repository, none in a lean one.
2. **A report subcommand** (deterministic, seconds): standing smells, orphaned units, fastest-growing directories since the last review, churny files, command names that overlap installed tooling, LOC by top-level directory; optionally pyscn clones and communities and vulture, pinned, on a `git archive` copy.
3. **A `harness-ops:architect` subagent**, read-only on code (Read, Grep, Glob, Bash), dispatched by the lead when the trigger fires. It reads the report, builds the feature inventory (§5 step 2), and files one proposal issue plus one issue per step (§5 steps 3-6), each with a measurable DONE WHEN that includes "inventory unchanged except approved items" and "test count not below baseline". It never edits, moves or deletes code; builders do, one issue each. It runs code-modernization-style rule extraction only on a flagged module or pair, never the whole repository.
4. **Two lines in `harness-ops:lead`**: dispatch the architect when orient says a review is due; feature cuts wait for the owner.
5. **Prevention, filed separately:** a scoped signature map pushed with each brief, and a warn-first check for new copies of existing code.

Bounds: the thresholds are judgement on four repositories, two of them a day or two old; recalibrate once H has a few hundred first-parent commits. No review was actually run at the fire points, and no experiment here shows that a pushed map changes Claude's reuse.
