Status: v0.2 (2026-11-20): DONE WHEN 1 of #587 (policy and API survey), and DONE WHEN 2 measured in section 7 (#622): 21 of 134 SOURCED paper claims, 12 of them abstract-only, still reach no open-access full text. Issue: #587
Labels: SOURCED = cited and fetched; VERIFIED = measured in this repo (script or command named); BELIEVED = judgment; ASSUMED = unchecked premise.
Search coverage: discovery 2026-11-20: OK (openalex, s2 answered). Two `dev lit search` queries on TDM licensing and LLM copyright found legal commentary but no study of publisher terms for AI agents; the question is contractual, so the sources are the publishers' own terms pages. Wiley's TDM page, Wiley's click-through licence, ACM's policy pages and Taylor & Francis's website terms answered HTTP 403 or 522 to the fetch tool, so those publishers are covered only in part (section 2.3, 2.5, 2.6).

## 0. Findings in one paragraph
Every publisher surveyed gives text-and-data-mining (TDM) access to subscribed full text for free to researchers at a subscribing institution, for non-commercial research only, through its own API and an individual key or token. None of them prices that access separately. None of them clearly permits what an AI agent does with a paper, which is to send its text to a model hosted by a third party. Elsevier is the only one with an AI clause aimed at agents. Its current API Service Agreement allows use "in combination with an AI System" only if the system "is used in a closed hosted (enterprise-grade) environment", "does not train the algorithm of an external AI System" and "does not share with and/or enable a third party access to the Elsevier content". Springer Nature's API terms forbid storing content "on any server other than an Internal Server" and letting "a third party to access or use any TDM Materials". IEEE's terms forbid "Uploading, transmitting, or otherwise providing Content to any AI system" for training or "incorporation into their datasets" without IEEE's written consent. Claude Code sends everything the model reads to Anthropic, which keeps it for 30 days under commercial terms, and keeps a plaintext transcript on the local disk for 30 days. Under a conservative reading, paywalled full text therefore must not enter an agent's context at all until the university library confirms otherwise in writing. The only institutional design these terms clearly allow is local-only: the owner runs the download by hand, the text stays in a local cache outside every repository, a deterministic matcher checks a quote against it, and the agent sees only a verdict and a locator. Rate limits differ by publisher: 1 request/s (Springer Nature), 3 articles/s and 60 requests per 10 minutes (Wiley, from Wiley's own client), 10 requests/s (Elsevier). Taking the strictest of each gives one serial request per second and at most 60 per 10 minutes, per publisher. Elsevier is the only one that sets a quote length: snippets of "up to 200 characters" with "a DOI link". Section 5 lists nine questions for the library. Whether the source is worth building at all is still DONE WHEN 2 (#9).

## 1. Question and scope
For each free TDM route to paywalled papers (Elsevier, Springer Nature, Wiley, IEEE, ACM, Taylor & Francis, Crossref text-mining links), this note records:

- who may use it (subscriber institution, registration, key, click-through);
- the permitted purpose;
- rate and volume limits;
- what may be stored, and for how long;
- how much may be reproduced in outputs, and the attribution required;
- whether an AI agent or automated pipeline is allowed.

It then maps each rule to a control in any design (section 4) and lists what only the university's own licence can answer (section 5). The owner's bar (#587, 2026-11-19) is full compliance with the publishers' terms and the university's licences. Where a term is ambiguous for an agent, the note says so and does not assume permission.

Out of scope: open-access routes (Unpaywall, CORE, PubMed, OpenAlex OA locations), which #9 adds, and the count of paywalled claims (DONE WHEN 2). Also out of scope is the law on TDM exceptions (EU DSM Directive Art. 3/4, UK s29A). Those exceptions do not override a contract the university signed, and reading them is the library's job, not an agent's (BELIEVED).

**How the quotes were read.** Each page was fetched on 2026-11-19 with Claude Code's WebFetch tool. That tool passes the page through a small model, which returns quotes of at most 125 characters. Fetching the raw HTML directly was refused by this environment's permission system. So the quotes below are the tool's extracts, not a byte-for-byte copy. Quotes that two separate fetches of the same page returned identically are marked "(twice)". A human spot-check against the live pages is follow-up F1. The terms quoted are public pages; no paywalled content was read.

### 2.1 Elsevier
- **Who and purpose.** Elsevier's policy "automatically enables researchers at subscribing academic institutions to text mine full-text content" and limits it to "non-commercial research purposes". Researchers must "self-register before receiving a personal API key", per [Text and data mining policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/text-and-data-mining) (Elsevier, undated, §Elsevier's policy) (SOURCED). The FAQ adds: "Researchers may download only content available under their institution’s subscription or other entitlement", per [Text and data mining FAQ](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/text-and-data-mining/faq) (Elsevier, undated, §How can I access the API?) (SOURCED). The API is free for this purpose: the API is "an easy and simple way for you to bulk download Elsevier content for non-commercial research text mining purposes", per [Text and data mining](https://www.elsevier.com/about/open-science/research-data/text-and-data-mining) (Elsevier, undated, §Why use an API?) (SOURCED).
- **Rate and volume.** The Article Retrieval API allows 10 requests per second with a weekly quota of 50,000, "Unlimited for Text Mining API Keys", per [API key settings](https://dev.elsevier.com/api_key_settings.html) (Elsevier Developer Portal, undated, table) (SOURCED). The FAQ says "there are no hard limits on the number of items that may be downloaded via our API" but "we reserve the right to deactivate any API key if we believe usage is abusive", per [Text and data mining FAQ](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/text-and-data-mining/faq) (§Are there limits on the amount of content I can download?) (SOURCED). The agreement asks that "You will avoid excessive and unnecessary use of the API Service that may cause a performance degradation", per [API Service Agreement](https://dev.elsevier.com/api_service_agreement.html) (Elsevier, CURRENT version, undated, cl. 2.1) (SOURCED).
- **Storage.** "Upon completion of the relevant text and data mining project, you will immediately and permanently delete all copies", per [Text and Data Mining provisions](https://dev.elsevier.com/tdm_service.html) (Elsevier, CURRENT, last revised 16 November 2023) (SOURCED). The agreement adds "You will promptly and permanently delete all copies (including back-up copies)" "of Elsevier content received via the API Service." with no stated trigger, per [API Service Agreement](https://dev.elsevier.com/api_service_agreement.html) (§4) (SOURCED; the missing trigger is library question Q7). "Your API key and an extracted corpus is personal to you and should not be shared with third parties" (twice), per [Text and data mining FAQ](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/text-and-data-mining/faq) (§If a third-party user wants to use a TDM corpus..., can I share it with them?) (SOURCED).
- **Output and quotes.** Output may include "snippets of up to 200 characters surrounding and excluding the text entity matched", and "Where snippets and/or bibliographic metadata are distributed, they should be accompanied by a DOI link". The notice "Some rights reserved. This work permits academic research purposes only, distribution, and reproduction in any medium," ... "provided the original author and source are credited." is also required, per [Text and data mining FAQ](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/text-and-data-mining/faq) (§How can I share parts of the TDM corpus in my output?) (SOURCED). The licence page forbids snippets "of more than 200 characters", per [Elsevier Text and Data Mining (TDM) License](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/text-and-data-mining/license) (Elsevier, undated, §does not allow you to) (SOURCED).
- **Automated access.** The licence also forbids "Use any robots, spiders or other automated downloading programs, algorithms or devices to search", per [Elsevier Text and Data Mining (TDM) License](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/text-and-data-mining/license) (§does not allow you to) (SOURCED). The 2017 provisions scope that ban to scraping Elsevier's sites "instead of using the APIs", per [Text and Data Mining provisions](https://dev.elsevier.com/tdm_service.html) (SOURCED). Scripted calls to the API are the intended route; scraping ScienceDirect pages is not (BELIEVED).
- **AI agents (the decisive clause).** The current agreement defines an "AI System" broadly. It covers anything that can "retrieve information, generate output, or make decisions, whether autonomously or semi- autonomously," "including enablers such as intelligent agents and multi-agents systems; Model Context Protocol (MCP) systems;" and "orchestration layers, proxies, caching layers,". "You may use the API Service (including but not limited to API access credentials and any Elsevier content)" "in combination with an AI System subject to the below conditions." The five conditions are:
  1. the system "is used in a closed hosted (enterprise-grade) environment solely for the individual scholarly or research use by the Authorized User";
  2. it "does not train the algorithm of an external AI System;";
  3. it "does not share with and/or enable a third party access to the Elsevier content, or any part thereof;";
  4. it "does not substantially or systematically reproduce, retain, store locally, redistribute or disseminate any Elsevier cont[ent]";
  5. it "does not create any derivative work and/or service based on the API Service".

  "You are responsible and liable for the use of any AI System that you enable (directly or indirectly) to access the API Se[rvice]". All of this is per [API Service Agreement](https://dev.elsevier.com/api_service_agreement.html) (Elsevier, CURRENT version, cl. 1.4, 2.4, 2.5) (SOURCED). The agreement's archived versions of 22 August 2025 and earlier have no AI clause, per the same page (SOURCED), so the clause is new since August 2025.
- **What 2.4 means for Claude Code (BELIEVED, library question Q2).** Claude Code itself is an "AI System" under 1.4: an agent that retrieves information and generates output. A devkit command it runs is an "orchestration layer". Condition 2 is met under commercial terms (2.8). Condition 3 is the problem: the model runs on Anthropic's servers, so whether Anthropic is a "third party" with "access to the Elsevier content" is open. So is whether that hosting is "a closed hosted (enterprise-grade) environment". Condition 4 bars an agent-side cache that "store[s] locally" Elsevier content. A cache kept by the owner's own TDM download, outside the agent, is ordinary TDM storage under the TDM provisions. The difference rests on who runs the download (control C2).

### 2.2 Springer Nature
- **Who and purpose.** "For subscribed journals and books, Springer Nature grants researchers text and data mining rights via their institutions" and "provided the purpose is non-commercial research". The rights are not automatic: subscribing institutions "may include text and data mining rights in all new and renewed Journal and ebook subscription agreements". "An API key can be requested for researchers who want to use Springer Nature’s TDM APIs." "TDM requirements from non-subscribers for pay-walled content are treated on a case-by-case basis." All per [Text and data mining](https://www.springernature.com/gp/researchers/text-and-data-mining) (Springer Nature, undated, §TDM for researchers at subscribing academic institutions; §Implementation by academic and government institutions; §TDM for non-subscribers) (SOURCED). Whether the university's licence includes TDM rights is library question Q1.
- **Rate.** "They are requested to limit this to 1 request per second.", per [Text and data mining](https://www.springernature.com/gp/researchers/text-and-data-mining) (§TDM for researchers at subscribing academic institutions) (SOURCED). The API terms add "You must stay within the specified rate limits for each API" and forbid circumventing them by "adding multiple accounts or keys", per [Additional terms for API users](https://dev.springernature.com/terms-conditions/) (Springer Nature, undated, §Use of APIs – general) (SOURCED).
- **Storage.** Researchers must "store content on a secure internal server without access for third parties and only for the duration of the TDM project." (twice), per [Text and data mining](https://www.springernature.com/gp/researchers/text-and-data-mining) (SOURCED). The API terms set three limits, all per [Additional terms for API users](https://dev.springernature.com/terms-conditions/) (§TDM on institutional license) (SOURCED):
  - downloads go "to a server only accessible to you" ", your institution, and other users at your institution who are “Authorized Users”" "(an “Internal Server”)";
  - storage "shall be limited" "to the duration of the TDM project";
  - no one may "(ii) allow a third party to access or use any TDM Materials", "(iii) perform TDM for a third party" "or (iv) store, or permit storage of, any content or TDM Materials on any server other than an Internal Server."

  After the institution's TDM License ends, "you shall destroy any copies of content", per [Additional terms for API users](https://dev.springernature.com/terms-conditions/) (§TDM on institutional license) (SOURCED).
- **Output and quotes.** TDM Output is "the data and information which is the result of any TDM" "excluding, however, any full-text duplication in whole or in part of the content on which the TDM is performed". "You may make the TDM Output available to third parties, for noncommercial use only". Both per [Additional terms for API users](https://dev.springernature.com/terms-conditions/) (§Definitions; §TDM on institutional license) (SOURCED). No snippet length is given. A verbatim quote is a "duplication ... in part", so it falls outside TDM Output. Whether a short scholarly quote is then allowed under ordinary quotation rules is library question Q4 (BELIEVED).
- **AI.** The API terms have no AI clause (SOURCED, same page, by absence). The reservation page says "Springer Nature explicitly reserves all rights in the Content for any kind of text-and-data-mining" "according to Article 4 (3) Directive (EU) 2019/790", including "any development, training, programming, improving or enriching of Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems", per [TDM Reservation policy](https://dev.springernature.com/tdm-reservation-policy/) (Springer Nature, undated) (SOURCED). Sending full text to a model host would store it on a server that is not an Internal Server (Anthropic keeps inputs 30 days, 2.8). That conflicts with clause (iv) on a plain reading (BELIEVED).

Its click-through TDM agreement (`olabout.wiley.com/WileyCDA/Section/id-826542.html`) answered HTTP 522 on two attempts. Neither was read. **Wiley's licence terms are a gap** (follow-up F1).
- What Wiley's own client says: "The TDM API token is a UUID issued via the TDM resources page". "API limits of up to 3 articles per second and 60 requests per 10 minutes" (attributed there to Wiley's TDM page). "Access will be determined via your public IP address" (twice). "only IP based access is supported". All per [WileyLabs/tdm-client](https://github.com/WileyLabs/tdm-client) (Wiley Labs, "Open Source from John Wiley & Sons, Inc. colleagues and friends", MIT, pushed 2026-10-16; README §Requirements, §Access denied) (SOURCED; organisation metadata VERIFIED with `gh api`, 2026-11-19).
- Wiley's stated AI position, as reported: AI developers "must obtain authorisation before using Wiley content for AI development, training, or implementation.", per [Wiley issues position statement on AI content "scraping"](https://www.researchinformation.info/news/wiley-issues-position-statement-on-ai-content-scraping/) (Research Information, 2025-04-29) (SOURCED; a trade-press report, not Wiley's terms). "Implementation" could cover an agent that reads Wiley full text at inference time (BELIEVED, library question Q8).
- Wiley sells a licensed route into Claude: "Anthropic's Claude, AWS marketplace, Mistral AI's Le Chat and Perplexity all connect to the AI Gateway", "an endpoint built on the Model Context Protocol (MCP)", "currently available to beta customers", per [Wiley Launches Interoperable Platform to Power Scientific Discovery in World's Leading AI Technologies](https://newsroom.wiley.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2025/Wiley-Launches-Interoperable-Platform-to-Power-Scientific-Discovery-in-Worlds-Leading-AI-Technologies/default.aspx) (Wiley, 2025-10-14) (SOURCED). If the university subscribes, that is the sanctioned route for Wiley content into an agent, not the TDM API (BELIEVED, Q8).

### 2.4 IEEE
- **Who and purpose.** "TDM is permitted for non-commercial research purposes only and requires an active IEEE Xplore institutional subscription.", per [Allowed API Uses](https://developer.ieee.org/Allowed_API_Uses) (IEEE, undated, §Text and Data Mining (TDM)) (SOURCED). "Access to the IEEE Xplore API is limited to current IEEE customers unless the requesting Licensee agrees to a" "specifc license in addition to these terms of use." [sic]. Use is for "non-commercial educational, research, or scientific activities within the Licensee's educational institution". Both per [IEEE Xplore API Terms of Use](https://developer.ieee.org/API_Terms_of_Use2) (IEEE, undated, §Grant of License) (SOURCED).
- **Keys, rate, storage, display.** All per [IEEE Xplore API Terms of Use](https://developer.ieee.org/API_Terms_of_Use2) (SOURCED):
  - "You must keep your API Keys confidential and not share them with any third party" (§6);
  - use is "limited to the number of calls per minute, hour, day, etc." shown at registration (§5, no public number);
  - "you shall not retain or store any Content for any reason once the License or these Terms are terminated" (§4(b));
  - "you may only present the Content in response to an individual query" (§4(a));
  - "you shall not distribute the Content in a form that is harvestable by humans or machines" (§4(e));
  - "you shall not use any robot, spider, site search/retrieval application" (§4(c)).
- **AI.** Under "Prohibited Use of Content", "This prohibition includes, but is not limited to, the following actions" "if conducted without the express prior written consent of IEEE". Two of the listed actions are "Uploading, transmitting, or otherwise providing Content to any AI system" ", Machine Learning system, or Large Language Model for training or incorporation into their datasets;" and "Using Content as a source, training set, or benchmark for any AI system". "Content" is "any and all proprietary content, data, information, or materials owned or controlled by IEEE that is the subject of this Agreement", which appears to include open-access content. All per [IEEE Xplore API Terms of Use](https://developer.ieee.org/API_Terms_of_Use2) (§Prohibited Use of Content, items 1-2) (SOURCED).
- **What it means (BELIEVED, Q6).** The upload ban is qualified by "for training or incorporation into their datasets". Inference under Anthropic's no-training commercial terms is arguably outside it. But "Using Content as a source ... for any AI system" has no such qualifier, and an agent that quotes a paper uses it "as a source". The conservative reading is that IEEE full text, including IEEE open-access articles fetched through this API, must not reach the model without IEEE's written consent.

`www.acm.org/publications/policies/dl_use_policies`, `libraries.acm.org/digital-library/policies` and ACM's January 2026 open-access bulletin all answered HTTP 403 to the fetch tool on 2026-11-19. ### 2.6 Taylor & Francis
"If you or your institution subscribes to content from Taylor & Francis", mining of subscribed and open-access content is allowed "without any additional charge, provided this is on a non-commercial basis." Researchers are asked to email "advising on your organisational affiliation (if any) and with a brief overview of your planned TDM activity." Use must "adhere to the terms and conditions outlined in the" STM model licence or a TDM supplement to the licence agreement. All per [Text and Data Mining](https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/textanddatamining/) (Taylor & Francis, undated) (SOURCED). The page names no API, rate limit, storage rule, output limit or AI clause. The website terms (`www.tandfonline.com/terms-and-conditions`) answered HTTP 403 and were not read. T&F access needs an email agreement first, so it is not a candidate for an automated source (BELIEVED).

### 2.7 Crossref text-mining links
Crossref records full-text links with an `intended-application` of `text-mining` and "the license under which content can be used". Access "might need a subscription or a login", or "you might need to accept a text and data mining license". The requirements "are at the discretion of the Crossref member and are not controlled by Crossref", and a link "does not guarantee access". "Rate limits may apply to websites on which the full text is hosted", signalled by the `CR-TDM-Rate-Limit` headers. All per [Text and data mining](https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/text-and-data-mining/) (Crossref, updated 2025-10-17) (SOURCED). Crossref's own click-through licensing service is gone: "we will retire the TDM click-through in December 2020", because "only 2 publishers have continued with the tool and fewer than 300 API tokens have been issued", per [Evolving our support for text-and-data mining](https://www.crossref.org/blog/evolving-our-support-for-text-and-data-mining/) (Vickery, Crossref blog, 2020-08-21) (SOURCED). So a Crossref TDM link is a pointer, not a permission. The publisher's licence (2.1-2.6) governs every download made through it (BELIEVED).

### 2.8 Where the text goes when an agent reads it
- "Claude Code runs locally. To interact with the LLM, Claude Code sends data over the network. This data includes all user prompts and model outputs". Commercial users (API, Team, Enterprise) have a "Standard: 30-day retention period". Anthropic "does not train generative models using code or prompts sent to Claude Code under commercial terms". Consumer plans train "when this setting is on". "Claude Code clients store session transcripts locally in plaintext under `~/.claude/projects/` for 30 days by default". Transcripts sent with `/feedback` "are retained for 5 years". All per [Data usage](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage) (Anthropic, Claude Code docs, fetched 2026-11-19, §Data training policy, §Data retention, §Telemetry services) (SOURCED).
- So any paywalled text that enters an agent's context is (a) sent to a third party's servers and kept there 30 days, (b) written to a local plaintext transcript, and (c) shipped for 5 years if anyone runs `/feedback` in that session. Each of these touches a rule above. Under consumer plans with training on, it also breaks Elsevier condition 2 (BELIEVED, follows from the quotes).
- Every Bash call an agent makes here runs with `CLAUDECODE` set (VERIFIED, `env | grep '^CLAUDE'` in this session, 2026-11-19). A devkit command can therefore refuse to download paywalled text when an agent runs it. That guards against accidents, not against an agent that unsets the variable (BELIEVED).

## 3. What the terms mean for an agent pipeline
- **Agreed by every publisher read (the cited claims of section 2):** free to a subscribing institution's researchers; non-commercial research only; a personal key or token that must not be shared; access only to entitled content; no scraping of the website instead of the API; delete at the end of the project or licence.
- **Not settled by any page (BELIEVED):** whether an AI agent may read subscribed full text through a model hosted by a third party. Elsevier allows AI systems only in "a closed hosted (enterprise-grade) environment" with no third-party access. Springer Nature allows storage only on an Internal Server. IEEE bars use "as a source" for an AI system without consent. Wiley's reported position requires authorisation for AI "implementation". No page says yes, and the most specific page (Elsevier) names agents, MCP and caching layers in its definition, so they are clearly in view. The conservative default is that paywalled full text never enters the model's context.
- **Quotes in a public repository:** only Elsevier sets a length (200 characters, with DOI link and a rights notice). Springer Nature's TDM Output excludes any duplication of the text. This repository and its consumers keep quotes in public notes, which is distribution. A cap of 200 characters with a DOI link is the most any page allows, and even that is not settled for Springer Nature or IEEE (Q4, Q6).

## 5. Questions for the university library
Owner, please ask the library these and record the answers (not the licence text) in the project's private notes. Until Q1-Q3 are answered yes in writing, the institutional source stays unbuilt (BELIEVED).

Springer Nature says only that subscriptions "may include" them. Does any institutional TDM agreement supersede the publisher's click-through or API terms?
2. **Q2 (agents).** May subscribed full text be read by an AI agent whose model is hosted by a third party (Anthropic, commercial terms, 30-day retention, no training)? Is that "a closed hosted (enterprise-grade) environment" with no "third party access" (Elsevier 2.4)? Is it storage "on any server other than an Internal Server" (Springer Nature)?
3. **Q3 (automation).** May an agent trigger the API downloads (Elsevier counts "orchestration layers" as part of an AI System), or must a person run them?
4. **Q4 (quotes).** May verbatim quotes of at most 200 characters from subscribed papers appear in a public GitHub repository? Does each one need Elsevier's "Some rights reserved" notice? Does Springer Nature's exclusion of "any full-text duplication in whole or in part" from TDM Output forbid such quotes, or do ordinary quotation rights apply?
5. **Q5 (purpose).** Is research recorded in a public repository by one researcher "non-commercial research" under each licence?
6. **Q6 (IEEE).** Is passing IEEE text, or short snippets of it, to an LLM with no training "using Content as a source ... for any AI system"? Does the university hold IEEE's written consent for any AI use?
