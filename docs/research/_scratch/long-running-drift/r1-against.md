# r1-against: the case against adding drift machinery (#282, round 1, adversarial seat)

Search coverage: `uv run dev lit status` on 2026-10-19 showed openalex (key), s2 (key), crossref, europepmc and arxiv up ("discovery: OK"). Also used: the Claude Code memory docs (WebFetch, 2026-10-19), read-only `git` on L1, L2, L3, `gh` on H, local transcripts of L1 (406 files incl. subagents, 2026-09-25..10-12), L2 (2 files, 10-16..17), L3 (26 files, 10-11..19) and repo-E (16 lead files). Code names only; no private text is quoted. Scripts are in [scripts/](scripts/) (`against_*`), all take paths as arguments; their private dumps stay in the scratchpad.

Coverage limit that bounds every finding below: transcripts exist only for the last 3 weeks of L1's 6 months, 2 days of L2 and 8 days of L3. Git covers the full histories. Drift that led to wrong work and was never noticed is invisible to a transcript search (survivorship), so "no incident found" is a lower bound on harm, not proof of none.

## Findings

**A1. Token cost of orient lines is negligible in dollars; that half of the cost argument fails (VERIFIED).** [against_orient_cost.py](scripts/against_orient_cost.py) over repo-E's 2026-10-11..16 window: 14 lead sessions, 3,251 lead calls (mean 232 per session). At the Opus 5.5 cache-read price used in [harness-efficiency.md](../../harness-efficiency.md) §2 ($0.20/MTok) and 1.33 tokens per word (ASSUMED), repo-E's whole orient output today (796 words, about 1.1k tokens) costs $0.69 per window ($0.05 per session). A 20-word line costs $0.02; printing all 11 L1 memory bodies (about 1,400 words) would cost $1.21; printing L1's 7,944-word HANDOFF $6.87. Orient sizes today: H 449 words, repo-E 796, L1's own orient script 8,743 words (it prints the HANDOFF). This agrees with [harness-efficiency.md](../../harness-efficiency.md) §6 ("text is cheap; conflicts and dead rules are the cost"). So the case against machinery must rest on build and maintenance cost, on warnings that go unread, and on checks that refuse correct work, not on tokens.

**A2. The machinery is the expensive part, and it does not get built (VERIFIED).**
- H: the 12 follow-ups filed by #220 on 2026-10-13 (#226-#236, #238) are all still open 6 days later; 2 are in progress (`gh issue view`, 2026-10-19). At about $15 API-equivalent per merged PR in repo-E ([harness-efficiency.md](../../harness-efficiency.md) §0), 12 more build PRs are on the order of $180 plus reviews (BELIEVED estimate; the per-PR figure is from another repository).
- L1 ran its own drift-surfaces research on 2026-10-04 and filed 11 drift tickets. At the last commit (2026-10-05) 1 is done, 1 in review and 9 sit in a 571-item backlog against 303 done (board directory listing; counts only). The project went idle with the machinery unbuilt.
- L1's one built piece of state-drift machinery, a HANDOFF-freshness check on its land command, refused 5 of 91 land or queue runs in the transcripts (2026-10-04..05), and in its first version it refused only after an 8-minute gate, which the lead's own retrospective listed as a re-run cost ([against_check_refusals.py](scripts/against_check_refusals.py), refusal text matched outside quotes; the same retrospective also logged land failures on a stale generated board file, fixed by having land render the file itself, i.e. generation instead of a check). The HANDOFF it forced grew from 176 to 7,944 words in 10 days over 121 revisions, 6.2% of all L1 commits, with 5 pruning revisions ([against_prose_churn.py](scripts/against_prose_churn.py)). A freshness check produced volume, not a short accurate state.

**A3. Standing warnings in orient are not acted on (VERIFIED here; SOURCED outside).**
- repo-E, from SessionStart output in transcripts ([against_lint_series.py](scripts/against_lint_series.py)): the `board lint` count shown at session start rose 72 (10-13) → 76 → 89 → 138 (10-16) → 134-138 (10-19); its daily maximum never fell. "Suite audit due (no audit recorded)" was shown 80 times and "Consolidation review due" 62 times from 10-13 to 10-19; across 705 commits since 10-11 no commit records either review, and orient still prints both today. H's `board lint` was 90 standing problems on 10-13 ([decision-records-and-drift.md](../../decision-records-and-drift.md) §4) and is 134 today.
- Outside evidence that volume and repeats, not time, drive dismissal: in 112 primary-care clinicians over 3.5 years, the odds of accepting a reminder fell 30% per additional reminder per encounter and 10% per 5-point rise in the share of repeated reminders; a newly deployed reminder showed no decline over time (Ancker et al. 2017, [Effects of workload, work complexity, and repeated alerts on alert fatigue in a clinical decision support system](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12911-017-0430-8), BMC Med Inform Decis Mak, retrospective cohort, abstract). Physicians in the top quartile of alerts seen in the prior 90 days responded far less to one alert (adjusted OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.35-0.42; 55,649 visits, 418 physicians; Murad et al. 2022, [JAMIA](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocac191), abstract). Pooled drug-interaction alert override is 90% (95% CI 85-95%, 16 studies, I² = 100%, so heterogeneity is extreme; Felisberto et al. 2024, [Health Informatics J](https://doi.org/10.1177/14604582241263242), abstract). In five projects using Coverity for at least five years, a median 36.7% of alerts were actionable, and even those took a median 96 days to fix (Imtiaz, Murphy and Williams, [ISSRE 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/issre.2019.00040), abstract). Developers named false positives and warning presentation as barriers to static-analysis use (Johnson et al., [ICSE 2013](https://doi.org/10.1109/icse.2013.6606613), 20 interviews, abstract).
- Contrary evidence: Ancker found no desensitization over time for a new alert, so a single new, rare, precise warning is not doomed. The risk is the pile: each added "due" or lint line joins one that is already ignored. No study found measures how coding agents (as distinct from humans) treat standing warnings in a session-start block (BELIEVED absence; one search, see Gaps).
- LLM side (SOURCED, model-dated): instruction-following degrades as instruction density rises; the best of 20 models reached 68% at 500 simultaneous instructions (Jaroslawicz et al. 2025, [arXiv 2507.11538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538), abstract; keyword-inclusion task, not coding). Every orient item is another instruction competing for the lead's attention, which is the cost A1 cannot see in dollars (BELIEVED transfer).

**A4. Drift caused little measured harm in the long-lived repositories (VERIFIED counts; codes BELIEVED, one rater).**
- Transcripts: [against_drift_encounters.py](scripts/against_drift_encounters.py) found 25 sentences where an agent remarked on stale or contradicting docs, memory or instructions (L1 19, L2 0, L3 6); hand-coded in [against_encounters_coded.csv](scripts/against_encounters_coded.csv), tallied by [against_tally.py](scripts/against_tally.py): 13 real drift, 3 refusals by drift machinery, 9 not drift. Of the 13: 10 cost nothing (noticed before acting: 5 by an exploring agent, 3 by an audit, 2 by the author in the same session), 1 cost one failed start (a copied script had drifted from its original), and 2 (one event: agent bodies and memory still describing a system the project had rewritten) cost one cleanup ticket at the rewrite. Only 1 of 13 involves auto memory.
- Owner prompts: [against_owner_prompts.py](scripts/against_owner_prompts.py) extracted 233 human prompts (L1 173, L2 43, L3 17). Read by hand, none corrects work caused by stale auto memory. One concerns a plan that omitted a component (an omission, not a stale fact), and one asks that the startup docs be kept current.
- Memory content: 14 entries across L1-L3 (L1 11, L2 1, L3 2). 10 are owner directives that carry their date and reason and go stale only by a later owner decision; L1's model-choice entry was updated in place the minute the owner changed that decision (file `modified` stamp equals the prompt time, 2026-10-05T01:33). 4 entries hold checkable facts (3 in L1, 1 in L3); every L1 fact (a local service setting, a dependency pin, a resource floor, a cache setting) was still true at L1's last commit (grep of the named script, hook and agent files), and two are already enforced by a hook or setting, so a stale entry would lose to the code anyway. The one L3 fact (hosting-plan limits) was not re-checked.
- Prose: the drift that did accumulate was volume. L3's CLAUDE.md grew 617 → 8,048 words in 2 months (49 revisions, 18.4% of commits), then one "prompt audit" day cut it to about 1,400 and a later rewrite to 1,094. L2's grew 611 → 2,585 with 0 pruning revisions. L1's original CLAUDE.md (4,435 words) was replaced wholesale ([against_prose_churn.py](scripts/against_prose_churn.py)). That is the pattern measured over 247,694 instruction lifetimes in 1,867 repositories: agent instruction files "grow without bound … stopping only when the repository retires or someone rewrites the file wholesale" (+226% over a lifetime, +4.9 net instructions per commit, deletion hazard falling with age; Chakrabarti 2026, [arXiv 2608.11095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11095), abstract). Drift machinery adds lines to that pile; it does not remove any.
- Rough balance (BELIEVED): observed drift harm in the transcript windows is about one failed command plus one cleanup ticket; the two observed fixes were single audit sessions (L3's cost at least $9.46 API-equivalent over 94 calls in 38 minutes, [against_session_cost.py](scripts/against_session_cost.py), output undercounted, so a lower bound). Against that, #220's 12 build issues are about $180 before reviews (A2), and L1's freshness check already cost re-runs.

**A5. Deletion and regeneration dominate maintenance where they were tried (VERIFIED).**
- L3 replaced its shipped specs, executed plans, roadmap and blockers file with one feature index plus a schema snapshot extracted from production: 89 files, +2,650 / -19,127 lines (`git show --shortstat`, commit dated 2026-10-18). The only drift it left was one dangling command reference in a doc written the next day, found by a review agent within a day (encounter 23), which is exactly what an exact reference check catches.
- The platform already ships the semantic sweep: `/doctor prompt-audit` "looks for problems such as instructions written for older models, references to files or commands that don't exist, and files that contradict each other" over CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, rules, skills, commands and subagents (Claude Code ≥ 2.1.283; [memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory#audit-your-instruction-files), fetched 2026-10-19). L1's and L3's two drift-fixing commits are both titled "prompt audit": +12/-14 lines in 7 files and +17/-17 in 5 files. Building a parallel semantic checker duplicates a maintained upstream feature.
- The platform also already loads the memory index into every main session: "The first 200 lines of `MEMORY.md`, or the first 25KB … are loaded at the start of every conversation", and when the index nears that limit Claude Code reminds Claude to "merge or drop stale entries" ([memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory), fetched 2026-10-19). Auto memory is machine-local and "isn't loaded into subagents". An orient listing of every memory entry (#220 check 5, [#233](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/issues/233)) therefore repeats, in the lead only, what the lead already sees.
- Write-time beats read-time for memory (SOURCED, model-dated): on 400 implicit-conflict scenarios the best evaluated model reached 55.2% at recognising that a memory was no longer valid, and models "often accept outdated assumptions"; the authors' baseline fix strengthens write-time revision (Chao et al. 2026, [STALE, arXiv 2605.06527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527), abstract). Listing entries again at read time does not address a gap the authors place between retrieving updated evidence and acting on it (BELIEVED reading).

**A6. The four classes are two mechanisms (BELIEVED, from A4's coding).** 12 of 13 coded drift events are a restated fact whose owner changed: memory (class 1) and prose of every kind (class 2) alike, including duplicated rules and copied scripts. One answer covers both: do not restate (link, generate, or put the fact in a test or hook), and check exactly the restatements that remain (paths, commands, flags). The other 1 of 13 is an external version moving (class 3), which shares its shape with template-plugin skew (class 4): a pinned or assumed version against a live one. Class 4 is an exact string comparison; class 3 has no exact form. Classes 1 and 2 need no separate memory machinery beyond the write-time rule.

## Per mechanism: risk, evidence, verdict

| mechanism (issue) | risk | evidence | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| orient lists every memory entry (#233, check 5) | duplicates the platform's own index load; another standing block in orient | A5 (docs), A4 (0 owner corrections from stale memory in 233 prompts; 4 checked facts true) | **reject as specified**. Instead: one HARNESS line, "when the owner changes a decision, edit or delete the memory entry in the same turn; promote rules into the repository and delete the entry" (write-time) |
| memory staleness or "no home" sweep | false positives on directives that are meant to live only in memory; one more ignored line | A3, A4 (10 of 14 entries are dated directives) | **adopt behind measure**: only if a re-measure on 3+ repositories finds a stale memory entry acted on |
| generated HANDOFF live state at orient (#233, check 6) | volume | #220 check 6: 19 of 19 hand claims went stale; A2 (L1 HANDOFF 7.9k words) | **adopt**, as generation that replaces prose, capped (for example 15 lines); never a freshness gate |
| HANDOFF or prose freshness gate | refusals of correct work, re-runs | A2 (5 of 91 L1 lands refused; earlier after an 8-minute gate) | **reject** |
| exact reference checks: paths, `uv run dev` commands and flags (#229) | low; exact | A4 (3 of 13 events are a stale command, flag or path), A5 (L3's one leftover) | **adopt** (existing checks; promotion rule of #220 stands) |
| semantic prose-vs-code sweep (LLM) | 57-75% precision in #220 §6; work orders on correct text | A5: `/doctor prompt-audit` exists | **reject building one**; run `/doctor prompt-audit` at each Claude Code or model upgrade and on a cadence, behind a measure (findings applied per run) |
| quote-presence sweep for upstream docs (#235) | a quote per comment to maintain; 0 events measurable on history | #220 §5 check 8 | **adopt behind measure**: only for comments that hard-code an upstream constant, and only after one real miss |
| template pin vs installed plugin skew (#163, class 4) | none if silent when equal | exact comparison; #282 body cites a real skew | **adopt**: one orient line printed only on mismatch |
| decision table markers, comment-decision lint (#226, #228) | more standing warnings | A3 | **adopt behind measure**: ship only with a measured action rate; a warning nobody acts on in 10 sessions is removed or becomes a gate |
| drift registry or bundle (L1 style) | backlog that is never built | A2 (L1: 1 of 11 built) | **reject** |
| standing "due" nags in orient | already ignored | A3 (80 and 62 displays, 0 actions) | **change**: print a warning only when it is new or its count rose since the last session; drop nags with no action in N sessions |
| delete instead of maintain: link don't copy, delete shipped specs and executed plans, regenerate indexes | loses history (git keeps it) | A4, A5 (L3: -19,127 lines; drift left: 1 reference) | **adopt** as the default for classes 1-2 |

## Gaps seen but not closed
- No transcripts cover L1's first 5 months or most of L2, so time-to-detection for months-old drift is unmeasured here; git-only proxies (fix commits naming a stale fact) are the pro seat's job.
- No study found on how coding agents respond to repeated session-start warnings; one `dev lit search` returned nothing on point. A small repo-E experiment (warnings printed only on change vs always, action rate per warning) would settle A3 for agents.
- The L3 hosting-plan memory fact was not re-checked; L1's and L3's prompt-audit sessions were priced only for L3.
- The encounter regex needs a doc-like subject and a staleness word in one sentence, so recall is unknown; one rater coded all 25.
- Whether L1's 8,743-word orient was read by subagents as well as the lead was not checked; if so, A1's L1 cost multiplies.
