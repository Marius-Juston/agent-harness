## 0. Findings in one paragraph
Do not ship ADRs: ship a smaller decision mechanism (tests and hooks, one checked decision table, a board-lint warning), and gate only the drift checks that were exact on history. In H's whole history (2026-10-11 to 13), 23 decisions were made in issue and PR comments; 5 (22%) exist only in comments and 6 changed an issue's scope without editing its DONE WHEN. Of 5 reversals, the one that needed a check (C1: the reason behind "never curl the literature APIs" deleted to fit a word budget, restored 8 h later) is caught by a literal marker on the reason. An ADR directory, path-scoped `.claude/rules/` and a decision-labelled issue index each prevent 0 of the 5 beyond that marker. The path-scoped rule was not even loaded when C1 happened: the deletion was a scripted Bash edit, and at most 19% of Bash writes in 242 transcripts were "cold" in the same way (an upper bound). Project B, which has ADRs, showed decay within 3 days: one ADR amended in place, one cited nowhere, and a research note that contradicted a policy ADR for about 14 hours until a research round, not the ADR, caught it. Agent evidence for decision records is real but weak and unreplicated, and it favours a short "why" next to the rule over a separate document. The minimum set is: (1) tests and hooks first (they already hold 10 of 23 decisions), plus a hook that denies direct curl, wget or WebFetch to the five literature API endpoints `uv run dev lit` wraps (replayed over 244 transcripts it blocks 236 direct calls and no article page); (2) one `.devkit/decisions.toml` table (rule, why-link, `enforced-by` literal on the reason, status), checked in about 30 lines and rendered as a linked, never-loaded index (while the check is a warning it stops a C1-style deletion only if the lead reads the warning at integrate; the hook holds C1's harm either way); (3) a `board lint` warning for scope decided in comments. Of 32 historical drift classes across the two repositories, today's checks would still miss 17. Replayed over every merge, three proposed checks raised zero false positives: deleted Owns paths under open issues (11 of 11 flags real, 1 root event) becomes a gate; `uv run dev` spans with flags and positionals (1 true event, 0 FP) stays a warning until a held-out replay, because its syntax rules were fitted on the same history; and integrate writes the test floor (9 raises, 0 false failures). DONE WHEN ticks and HANDOFF live state should be generated, not checked (0 ticked boxes in 451 body versions; prose checks of HANDOFF were 20% precise). Markers and `.py` spans stay warnings, decision-level drift needs an advisory step beyond markers, and links go to a weekly sweep with a fixed lychee config (0 of 539 reported URLs were verifiably dead). Every precision rests on 0 or 1 real events and was measured in-sample, so no warning becomes a gate before a held-out replay over the next 30 merges.

## 1. Question and scope
The questions ([QUESTION.md](_scratch/adr-drift/QUESTION.md)):
1. Should agent-harness and its template ship ADRs, and if so with what format, trigger, discovery, supersession and gate check?
2. What drifts at each level of a harness-run repository, what does each level have today, and what is the cheapest reliable enforcement?
3. (Owner, before round 2.) Is there something better than ADRs for an agent-run repository? Candidates: executable decisions, path-scoped rules, hooks, inline rule plus reason plus `enforced-by` marker, decision-labelled issues with a generated index, and ADRs as the baseline. Criteria: reversals prevented, words in agent context, new drift surface, maintenance, false-positive risk.

Data: H's whole history (155 issues, 67 PRs, 69 first-parent merges through `635a833`) and project B (150 issues, 56 merged PRs, 56 merges). Free options only. One rater coded every decision and every flag, with no second coder, so the codings are BELIEVED and the counts that follow from them are VERIFIED.

## 3. ADRs and the alternatives
### 3.1 Evidence on ADRs with human teams (SOURCED)
- Adoption is low, and about half of GitHub repositories with ADRs hold only 1 to 5, "tried but not yet definitively adopted"; systematic use is a team activity over a long period (Buchgeher et al., IEEE Access 2023, [Using Architecture Decision Records in Open Source Projects—An MSR Study on GitHub](https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2023.3287654), abstract).
- In a sample of 5,435 of the 116,211 AIDev agent-active repositories, ADR or decision-log directories appear in 0.9-1.9% of repositories per visibility stratum, which the authors call lower bounds (Díaz et al. 2026, [Methodological Harness in Agentic Software Engineering: An Empirical Study on Mining Software Repositories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32014), preprint; the rates are in the full text's Results table, read through the arXiv MCP, not in the abstract).
- In 4,316 ADRs from 547 projects, "alternatives, decisions drivers, and some quality concerns remain under-documented" (Miccio Palermo et al. 2026, [A Text Mining and Classification Approach for Analyzing Architecture Decision Records](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07375), preprint).
- A 3-month industrial introduction improved documentation culture but left distributed-systems problems open, and where records are stored shaped their usefulness ([Ahmeti et al., ECSA 2024](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/542849), abstract).
- Nygard's format beat MADR on comprehension in a student experiment (Nogueira et al. 2026, [One Size Fits All? An Empirical Comparison of ADR Templates regarding Comprehension, Usability, and Ease of Adoption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27333), preprint, N not in the abstract). Formats: [Nygard 2011](https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions) and [MADR](https://github.com/adr/madr/blob/develop/template/adr-template.md).
- No study found measures how often recorded decisions are reversed or re-argued, for humans or agents (BELIEVED absence, from the round-1 searches).

### 3.2 Evidence with coding agents (SOURCED; all 2026, model-dated, none replicated)
- With ADRs served over MCP, one 12.5-turn TypeScript build took 10.1% less time and wrote 16-25 E2E tests where the plain run wrote 0; one project, the tool's author, no statistics ([Kitayama, TechRxiv](https://doi.org/10.36227/techrxiv.177205025.54351571/v1), abstract).
- Retrieval of recorded product decisions raised Claude Code's decision compliance from 46% to 95% over 8 tasks, but the decisions were designed by the authors to be invisible in code and there is no read-all-ADRs baseline ([Dillon and Varanasi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08112), §7.6).
- A pre-registered study of a versioned decision file found +26 pp localization for a small executor given context from a stronger model, and a frontier gain of +14.6 pp that lost significance after Holm correction (p = 0.081) ([Kerbel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3843282.3844425), abstract).
- Contrary: over 288 runs of Claude Code and Codex on 17 real tasks, context files did not change correctness, bounded to at most 10-15 pp by equivalence testing ([Khatri 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27250), abstract).
- LLM checks of ADR compliance were weakest on organizational and process decisions, F1 0.71-0.79, on 980 ADRs from 109 repositories (Su et al., ICSA 2026, [Evaluating Large Language Models for Detecting Architectural Decision Violations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07609)). H's at-risk decisions are of that kind.
- Rationale comments removed 99.3% of excess instructions in synthetic worlds, and the deletion hazard of agent instructions falls with age (log-hazard -0.032 per commit, 95% CI -0.047 to -0.019) over 247,694 instruction lifetimes ([Chakrabarti 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11095)).
- An added rule in an Agent Skill raised the rate at which four coding agents took the required action by +0.23 on average, mainly for rules that name a command or path; when the Skill body loads only on demand, as in real tools, the agents kept about half (51%) of that gain (Wang et al. 2026, [Agent Skill Evolution: How Revisions Affect Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04832), abstract).
- Weak evidence for ADRs as agent guardrails: a design-science study that used executable requirements, C4 models and ADRs reports less implementation drift than unstructured or prompt-engineered development of one web application (Lipsanen et al. 2026, [Shift-Up: A Framework for Software Engineering Guardrails in AI-native Software Development -- Initial Findings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20436), abstract; exploratory, no numbers in the abstract).
- BELIEVED synthesis: the benefit sits on decisions an agent cannot infer from code (owner policy, rejected alternatives, the reason for a rule). That favours a short reason next to the rule, in the file the agent edits, over a separate document read on demand.

### 3.3 Path-scoped rules miss the edit that mattered (SOURCED docs; VERIFIED probe)
- A rule with `paths` loads when Claude uses Read, Write or Edit on a matching file, or a Bash read such as `cat`; an invalid glob matches nothing silently; and rules are "context, not enforced configuration" ([memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory#path-specific-rules)).
- Probe on Claude Code 2.1.296 ([r2_rules_probe.sh](_scratch/adr-drift/scripts/r2_rules_probe.sh)): loaded on Read, `cat`, `sed -n` and Write, and inside a subagent; not loaded on Grep, `python3 -c "open(...)"` (the same class as `uv run dev code show`) or a denied Read. Haiku ignored a loaded rule in 4 of 7 loads as text "from the tool result"; Opus followed it in 3 of 3.
- In 242 transcripts ([r2_rule_triggers.py](_scratch/adr-drift/scripts/r2_rule_triggers.py)), 32% of reads (2,006 of 6,211) would load a rule, and 118 of 619 Bash writes with an extractable target (at most 19%) came before any loading access. The Bash-write heuristic was right on 16 of 30 sampled matches, so these are upper bounds.
- C1 was cold: the builder's only earlier access to the file was `grep -n`, the deletion was a `python3` heredoc that then ran the gate's ratchet check, and its first loading read came 62 s later ([r2-mechanisms M3](_scratch/adr-drift/r2-mechanisms.md)). BELIEVED: even a loaded rule would have lost to a failing word-budget gate; only another gate check changes that outcome.

That supports a generated, linked index over a hand-kept directory, not an ADR layer (BELIEVED).
- Conformance checks are "often not automated" because tool-specific specifications cost too much to set up and maintain (Caracciolo et al., WICSA 2015, [A Unified Approach to Architecture Conformance Checking](https://doi.org/10.1109/wicsa.2015.11), abstract). One table read by one check keeps upkeep at one row per decision (BELIEVED).

### 3.5 Answer: no ADRs; the minimum mechanism (BELIEVED, from 3.1-3.4)
1. **Test or hook first** for any decision code can check. Add the missing test for D02 (no personal data in literature queries) and a PreToolUse hook that denies Bash `curl`/`wget` and WebFetch to the endpoints `uv run dev lit` wraps (§5, check 9), with a reason naming `uv run dev lit` and allow-list tests. The hook holds C1's harm whatever the prompt says.
2. **One decision table**, `.devkit/decisions.toml`. Each row: `id`, `rule` (one line), `why` (an issue, comment or note link), `enforced-by = [{path, literal}]` and `status = active | superseded` (with `by` when superseded). The literal is 5 words or fewer and taken from the **reason**, not the rule wording (§5, check 7). The check reports rows examined (zero fails per `ran.py`) and flags an active literal missing from its path. Removing one means flipping the row in the same PR, which makes the reversal explicit. While the check is a warning (§7), a C1-style deletion is stopped only if the lead reads the warning at integrate. `uv run dev docs` renders `docs/generated/decisions.md`, linked from HARNESS and never imported. The template ships an empty table and the check.
3. **A `board lint` warning** when an open issue's newest lead or owner comment is newer than its last body edit and says DONE WHEN, scope or decided. Scope decided in a comment then has to be written into the body.
4. **Not now:** a template ADR directory (no reversal prevented beyond the marker, and the largest drift surface) and path-scoped rules for decisions. A project that keeps ADRs for owner policy points a row's `why` at the ADR, and the table enforces it. The unique ADR numbers of [#63](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/issues/63) are then a project concern, not a template one.

## 5. Each proposed check, replayed over history (VERIFIED, [r2-replay](_scratch/adr-drift/r2-replay.md))

A flag is TRUE when someone later had to change text, issues or code because of it. The bar for a gate is zero false positives on history: Google's compiler checks require "no effective false positives", and review checks tolerate up to 10% ([Sadowski et al., CACM 2018](https://6826.csail.mit.edu/2020/papers/google-analysis-cacm.pdf); SOURCED).

- **1a** caught the one real CLI drift on record: `uv run dev board block <n> "<why>"` in builder.md and a hint string, present for 20 merges over 12 h 48 min (fixed in [PR #151](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/pull/151)). The positional parse caught it; the flag-only check r1 proposed caught nothing in the whole history. Five syntax rules (loosen required arguments, accept `a|b` and globs, `...` as elision, cut at shell operators, `N` and `<x>` as placeholders) cut 127 parser errors to 0 FP, but they were fitted on this history. Because the rules were fitted on the history they are scored on, 1a ships as a warning and becomes a gate only after the held-out replay, like the markers. It covers 1 of the 3 historical CLI findings; [#179](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/issues/179) (`--help` hid a mode) and [#188](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/issues/188) (a message named an option the entry point does not read) need per-entry-point tests.
- **2**: H had 0 flags in 3,812 (path, commit) checks. The integrate-time rule is coded in [r2_owns_replay.py](_scratch/adr-drift/scripts/r2_owns_replay.py) (`RESULT-AT-MERGE`): at each merge, an issue open at the merge, not closed by the merged PR (a closing keyword in its body, or the `<issue>-<slug>` branch name), that owns a path the merge deletes. In project B it flags 11 issues at 1 merge (its migration): 6 closed by hand within 15 minutes as moved, 2 done but left open (closed 30 h later), and 3 that still own deleted paths 58 h later. These are in-sample numbers. No warning becomes a gate until a held-out replay over the next 30 first-parent merges of each repository shows zero false positives.

## 6. Why no semantic (LLM) drift check (SOURCED; BELIEVED)
- The best measured just-in-time README drift detector reached 75% precision (244 TP, 81 FP over 6,000 commits, 1.35 false alerts per 100 commits), and 26% of its true bugs were external references that change with no commit ([READU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15780), Table II).
- A configuration-smell study measured 93.5% precision for a syntactic smell and 57% for the semantic "Conflicting Instructions" (dos Santos et al., SCAM 2026, [Configuration Smells in AGENTS.md Files: Common Mistakes in Configuring Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15828)).
- BELIEVED: an agent treats a failing check as a work order, so a false alert means editing correct text or weakening the check, the gaming pattern of [agent-harness.md §4](agent-harness.md#4-gaming-and-integrity-sourced-verified-in-repository-a). Semantic judges are sweep inputs at most, re-measured whenever the model changes. No study found measures how agents react to false-positive doc checks.

## 7. Recommendation for this project
Build issues are listed in Follow-ups. Levels and bounds (BELIEVED unless marked):
1. **No ADR directory** in agent-harness or the template. Decisions go to a test or hook when code can check them, else to a row of `.devkit/decisions.toml` with a reason marker of 5 words or fewer. Seed the table with the 6 prose-only rules (D01, D05, D07, D08, D11, D12; [r2-mechanisms M1](_scratch/adr-drift/r2-mechanisms.md)). The check starts as a warning printed by the gate and by `uv run dev integrate`; it becomes a gate after the held-out replay.
2. **Hook** denying `curl`/`wget`/WebFetch to the five endpoint prefixes of check 9, with allow-list tests for article pages and Europe PMC full text and an override for work on `devkit/lit.py`. Its effect is measured with the re-run in [#174](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/issues/174): at most 1 run in 20 per agent type calls a literature API directly.
3. **Gate now** (0 FP in-sample, no fitted rules): check 2 in `uv run dev integrate` (refuse a merge deleting a path that an open issue other than its own owns) and as a `board lint` error.
4. **Generated now**: integrate writes the floor raise (0.95 x passed when below 0.90 x passed); `uv run dev pr --partial` copies the DONE WHEN checklist and integrate writes the ticks back by exact text; `uv run dev orient` prints every memory entry and the live state of each issue or PR that HANDOFF's In flight, Next and Waiting sections name.
5. **Warnings**: check 1a in `check_dev_commands` and check 7 until promoted, check 1b (`.py`), and the `board lint` comment-decision warning. Drop 1c as a gate and 1d entirely.
6. **Decision level** (advisory): an auditor or sweep step that checks notes against the decision table, and a table search in `board new` (#238).
7. **Sweeps**: a weekly lychee workflow in agent-harness, with the template's config changed to exclude `doi.org` (cite-check already resolves DOIs) and keep the job token for GitHub; and a quote-presence sweep for doc URLs cited in code.
8. **Promotion rule**: a warning becomes a gate only after 30 held-out merges per repository with 0 FP; a gate that produces one FP drops back to a warning.

What would change this: a measured re-litigation rate of owner-policy decisions above about 1 in 10 decision-touching runs; owner decisions with rejected alternatives that agents keep re-proposing despite a table row (then add a short ADR body behind `why`); or a Claude Code change that loads path rules on Bash writes and Grep.

## 8. Limits
- One rater for every coding (decisions, reversals, mechanism calls, flags); no inter-rater agreement.
- H's history is 3 days and 69 merges; no decay rate can be measured on it.
- Transcripts, memory and the probe come from one machine; the probe's n is small (Haiku 7 loads, Opus 3) and it did not use the harness's real prompts.
- Kitayama, Kerbel, Buchgeher and Ahmeti are abstract-only; their per-condition run counts are unverified.
- The URL re-check could not judge 26 URLs (bot walls, TLS failures from this machine).
