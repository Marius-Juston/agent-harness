## 1. Question and scope
#262 asks whether the researcher keeps its accuracy at lower cost at effort `medium`, or with a Sonnet 5.5 search-and-source helper, against Opus 5.5 at `high`, the setting since 2026-10-19. Researchers were 50 % of the measured window's output-corrected spend, $1,157 of $2,314 (SOURCED, [r2-research-and-lead-cost.md](_scratch/harness-efficiency/r2-research-and-lead-cost.md) §1). The owner's goal is high effectiveness at low token use, and the experiment's own cost must stay small: $15 of the owner's API budget for this issue at pre-registration, since 2026-11-20 $25 for #262 and #590 together (PROTOCOL.md amendment 1).

## 2. What outside evidence says (SOURCED, one vendor)
All from [Optimizing for cost and intelligence](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence) (Anthropic, Claude Platform docs, fetched 2026-11-19). The page now has footnotes that #254 did not have.
- **Effort, coding.** SWE-bench Pro, footnote 3: "Measured against `high`, Claude Opus 5.5 scored about 2.5 points lower at its default, `medium`, for about 70% of the cost per task". Two runs per setting.
- **Effort, defect search.** Footnote 8: "Alone, Claude Opus 5.5 scored 2.3 points higher at `high` than at `medium` (0.7 to 3.9)", and "cost 15% less at `medium` than at `high`" (0.827 at $200 against 0.850 at $236).
- **Effort, research.** Footnote 21 (DRACO, 100 tasks, 3 attempts each): "At `low` effort, it scored 13.3 points lower on DRACO, 3.4 on HLE, and 14.0 on the physics set than at `medium`". The body says effort on research "bought a lot in one measurement and a little in another" and "On research tasks, measure the model you ship". No Opus 5.5 `high` arm was run on DRACO, so `medium` against `high` for research stays unmeasured.
- **Model, research.** Footnote 7 (DeepResearch Bench II, 44 tasks): "Claude Opus 5.5 over Claude Sonnet 5.5, 2.4 (1.0 to 3.8)" rubric points, at "1.5 times the cost per task"; with refused or stopped attempts counted as 0 on all 50 tasks, Opus is "not measurably ahead". The judge is an Anthropic model, which "may favor the house style".
- **Sonnet subagents.** Footnote 8 (defect search, Opus orchestrator at `high`): "Claude Opus 5.5 with Claude Sonnet 5.5 subagents scored 0.4 points higher than Claude Opus 5.5 alone at `medium` (1.1 lower to 1.9 higher), 1.9 lower than alone at `high` (0.6 to 3.2)", at $139 against $200 and $236. This is not research, and the subagents were called once per part by design.
- **Helpers may go unused.** In the DRACO team arm the lead "started a helper in only 5 of 300 attempts", so a helper arm must check that delegation happens (the pilot's `agent_calls`).

Two more sources, outside the vendor page:
- [How we built our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) (Hadfield et al., Anthropic Engineering, 2025-06-13): an Opus 4 lead with Sonnet 4 subagents "outperformed single-agent Claude Opus 4 by 90.2%" on Anthropic's internal research eval, and on BrowseComp "token usage by itself explains 80% of the variance". Older models, internal eval: it supports the helper shape, not its price (SOURCED).
- [Who is the Agent to Blame? Localizing Faithfulness and Citation Mistakes in Agentic Deep Research](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1681.pdf) (Hirsch, Wan, Wang, Stengel-Eskin, Bansal and Dagan, EMNLP 2026), abstract: "Almost every agent makes a lot of mistakes with the exception being those that summarize a single document", and "84.7% of final-report errors in AI-Q originate at the orchestrator". So the cheap place for a smaller model is single-document extraction, and the orchestrator that cites should stay on the stronger model, which is arm C's shape (SOURCED, abstract only, three open-source systems; single-source).

## 3. The pre-registered design (VERIFIED: committed before any trial, `1d7b83f`)
[PROTOCOL.md](_scratch/researcher-effort/PROTOCOL.md) and [questions.yaml](_scratch/researcher-effort/questions.yaml) hold the full text; the parts that decide the result:
- **Tasks.** Q01-Q10 are round-1 sub-questions from four audited notes (test-suite design, architecture consolidation, fix-now vs defer, decision records), chosen because they rest on literature and public docs only. Q11 and Q12 are spares. Measurement sub-questions (transcripts, sibling repositories) are excluded: a clone cannot reproduce them and the data is private.
- **No peeking.** Each trial runs in a remote-less clone with the question's slug folder, its note, the INDEX.md row and the experiment's own folder deleted and the deletion committed. The gold files date from 2026-10-12..20, after the model's training cutoff (BELIEVED, so no recall from training). A trial that reads a hidden path or fetches this repository's research notes from GitHub is flagged ([summarize.py](_scratch/researcher-effort/scripts/summarize.py) `leaks`).
- **Arm C.** [arm_c_agents.py](_scratch/researcher-effort/scripts/arm_c_agents.py) builds `researcher-helper` (the shipped researcher prompt plus one delegation paragraph) and `research-scout` (Sonnet 5.5, read-only search and fetch tools, extracts with verbatim quotes, no files), passed with `--agents`. The researcher re-opens any source it quotes for a finding. Arm C runs its orchestrator at `medium`, whereas the vendor's subagent result in §2 had it at `high`.
- **Metrics.** The primary metric is audited-claim accuracy: a blind research-auditor scores 10 sampled claims, counting supports as 1 and partly as 0.5. Also measured: cite-check problems, recall of the gold file's cited works, and $ and tokens by kind and model, with cache writes priced apart. A note is accepted at accuracy ≥ 0.80 with 0 cite-check problems, and cost is reported as $ per accepted note.
- **Decision.** The cheapest arm whose mean accuracy is at most 5 points below the best wins; within 10 % on cost, fewer parts win (B, A, C). The question-bootstrap lower bound is reported but does not decide.

## 4. Cost and power of the full stage (VERIFIED, [power_and_cost.py](_scratch/researcher-effort/scripts/power_and_cost.py))
- **Cost.** 10 questions × 3 arms × 3 trials = 90 researcher runs + 90 audits. That is **$339-389** at the repo-E round-1 median ($2.84 a run), **$419-468** at the round-2 median ($3.72) and **$486-535** at the all-run mean ($4.47); each range runs from this machine's one auditor run ($0.93) to repo-E's auditor cost ($1.48). Arms A and B alone cost **$226-357**. The per-run inputs are repo-E's (SOURCED, [r2-research-and-lead-cost.md](_scratch/harness-efficiency/r2-research-and-lead-cost.md) §1). This repository's own 9 researcher runs on this machine average $6.44 transcript-priced, or about $2.64 after `dev agent-stats --cost` scales them to the client's cost records (VERIFIED, 2026-11-19).
- **Power.** The power runs simulate 10 questions, 3 trials and 10 claims, with base accuracy 0.75-0.85 and question spread 0.08 (both ASSUMED, since no per-note audit rate is recorded). When the arms are truly equal, the 5-point point rule keeps the cheaper arm 84-95 % of the time. When the cheaper arm is truly 10 points worse, the rule still picks it 4-14 % of the time. A one-sided 95 % non-inferiority bound would clear −5 points only 39-58 % of the time even with equal arms. That is why the point rule decides, and a winner whose bound falls below −0.10 gets a live follow-up (PROTOCOL.md §5).
- **What a win is worth.** At the vendor's cost ratios, `medium` would save $174-347 on the window's $1,157 researcher line (7.5-15.0 % of all spend). An Opus orchestrator with Sonnet subagents would cost 0.589 of Opus alone at `high`, which is $476 (20.6 %), if research behaved like defect search (VERIFIED arithmetic on SOURCED ratios from [Optimizing for cost and intelligence](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence) footnotes 3 and 8; quality not modelled). The full stage ($339-535) costs one to three windows' worth of the `medium` saving. These are the pre-registration's repo-E prices; the pilot re-prices the full stage at $164-213, or $107-140 for A and B (§5.1, §7).

### 5.2 Delegation in arm C (VERIFIED, [count_refetch.py](_scratch/researcher-effort/scripts/count_refetch.py), output [pilot/C/refetch.json](_scratch/researcher-effort/pilot/C/refetch.json); the transcripts stay on the owner's machine)
The helper arm did delegate: two `research-scout` calls, 23 helper tool calls (9 of them source fetches) and $0.21 of Sonnet. That is unlike the DRACO team lead, which started a helper in 5 of 300 attempts (§2). The Opus thread then made 9 fetches itself, 7 abstracts and 2 pages; 8 of those 9 (6 of its 7 distinct sources) were sources a scout had already opened. C's prompt asks for re-opening: "re-open a source yourself (`uv run dev lit get`, WebFetch, the arxiv MCP) before quoting it for a claim that drives a finding" ([arm_c_agents.py](_scratch/researcher-effort/scripts/arm_c_agents.py), `DELEGATE`). C's Opus spend was $0.66 against B's $0.56, and most of that gap is cache writes ($0.30 against $0.18), which taking in two scout reports would also produce, so this run does not isolate the re-fetches as the cause (BELIEVED). That re-open rule is also the safeguard §2 motivates: 84.7 % of AI-Q's final-report errors start at the orchestrator (Hirsch et al.). Arm C stays as pre-registered. With n = 1 this is one run, not an arm property; stage 1 should report C's Opus re-fetches and cache writes per trial as a measurement to watch.

### 5.3 Output quality without an auditor (VERIFIED, [score_pilot.py](_scratch/researcher-effort/scripts/score_pilot.py), `cite-check.txt`)
- **cite-check problems:** 0 in every arm (A 16 citations, B 12, C 9).
- **Gold-work recall:** A 9/35 (0.257), B 7/35 (0.200), C 6/35 (0.171). The three arms together found 12 of the 35 gold works, and 3 of them in all arms: the Cursor difference-in-differences study, the Code Red paper and the agentic-refactoring study. A alone found 3 (a code-churn paper, a clone paper and a code-maintainability study), B alone 1 and C alone 1. Recall rewards finding what the original researcher found, and one question cannot separate arms.
- **Quote grounding** is a proxy, not the protocol's accuracy. It is the share of quotes of 25 characters or more that appear verbatim in some tool result the trial saw: A 35/35, B 13/13, C 15/16. C's one miss is an editorial bracket, "target[] refactoring" for the source's "targeting refactoring", not an invented quote. Grounding cannot tell whether a quote supports its claim, which is what the auditor measures.
- **Accuracy and $ per accepted note:** not measured. There was no auditor run in stage 0, so no answer can be "accepted" under §4 of the protocol.

### 5.4 Did the path work end to end? Yes, with three defects to fix before stage 1 (VERIFIED, transcripts)
- **Worked.** The steps ran without manual repair: clone, hide and commit; headless `claude -p` on the API key with the plugin and, for arm C, the `--agents` file; the budget cap; the copied answer; `summarize.py`; `cite-check`. The first attempt (04:17Z) got a 401 and ran nothing ($0, kept in the ledger and marked). User-level MCP servers and rules on the owner's machine loaded into every trial. That is the same for all arms and is also the real environment, but it is not a clean one.
- **Defect 1, gold reachable through git history.** The clone keeps history, and the default allowlist has `git log*` and `git show*` ([devkit/evals.py](../../devkit/evals.py) `DEFAULT_TOOLS`). Arm C ran `git log --oneline -3 -- docs/research/architecture-consolidation.md`, which returned only three commit subjects, not the note (flagged in `leaks`; no gold text was read). But `git show HEAD~1:<path>` would have printed the gold, so stage 1 needs a history-free clone.
- **Defect 2, math checks denied.** The allowlist has no `uv run python` or `python3`, so every arm's check script was refused (main-thread result-event denials: A 8, B 6, C 4; C's own answer reports six more `lit search` calls by its scout that were denied and are not in that count). All three answers then said so and labelled the numbers BELIEVED instead of VERIFIED. That is honest, but it removes the "check the math" step from every arm.
- **Defect 3, missing arxiv tools.** The allowlist names four arxiv tools, but the researchers also called `search_paper_text` and `download_paper`, and both were denied. One compound `for` loop of `dev lit search` calls was also denied in each arm; in C, the scout fell back to WebSearch.

## 6. What blocked the runs on 2026-11-19, and what `dev eval` lacks (VERIFIED, that session)
- `uv run dev eval --help` was refused by the worktree-isolation guard, because the string contains `eval`. The guard reads the subcommand name as a shell `eval`. The source shows `--model`, `--effort` and `--agent` are all present ([devkit/evals.py](../../devkit/evals.py) `command`), so no pass-through is missing.
- The first paid call was a Haiku probe with `--max-budget-usd 0.05`, meant to check that the API key bills and that `--agents` loads. The auto-mode permission classifier denied it ("Unauthorized Access"). Per the lead's rule, it was not retried another way. $0 was spent that session; the owner ran stage 0 by hand on 2026-11-20 (§5).
- `dev eval` cannot run this experiment as it stands, so the pilot is a hand-run script. Four things are missing: it cannot hide paths in the clone, it deletes the clone together with the file the researcher wrote, it has no per-trial budget cap, and it has no per-arm `--agents` file. These are filed as #604 next to #590, which owns `devkit/evals.py` and builds the research eval's auditor scoring. The scripts here were tested on a synthetic transcript (cost, tokens, delegation, leak flag) and on gold-vs-gold recall (1.0) and note-vs-round-file recall (0.40). The clone, hide and commit steps were run once, unpaid, and worked.

## 7. Recommendation for this project
1. **Keep `effort: high` on the researcher.** The pilot's B/A cost ratio of 0.58 is one draw with no accuracy attached. The vendor's effort cost on research is unmeasured at `medium`-`high`, and it is 13.3 points at `low`-`medium`, so switching on a guess is not safe on the 50 % line (BELIEVED from §2 and §5).
2. **Do not spend the remaining $22.34 on a cut-down stage 1.** Within the remaining budget, arms A and B with one audit per trial fit only 3 questions × 1 trial ($10.72-14.02) or 5 × 1 ($17.86-23.36), and #590 needs part of the same $25. At those sizes the 5-point rule keeps a truly equal `medium` 66-69 % of the time and a truly 10-point-worse `medium` 29-36 % of the time. The pre-registered 10 × 3 design gets 88-94 % and 10 % at trial spread 0.08, the same spread as the small designs (84-95 % and 4-14 % over both spreads, §4) (VERIFIED, [pilot_arith.py](_scratch/researcher-effort/scripts/pilot_arith.py), 400 seeded simulations per cell, ASSUMED accuracy 0.75-0.85). A result that wrong a third of the time would not justify changing the setting.
3. **Optional, $2.79-4.44: audit the three pilot answers** with the research-auditor (Opus 5.5 `high`, blind, PROTOCOL.md §4). It is the only cheap step that adds information. It runs the auditor path end to end, gives this repository's audit cost, and gives the first accuracy readings (n = 1 per arm, still no verdict). It should run only if #590's baseline leaves room in the $25.
4. **Fix the three stage-0 defects first** (#655: history-free clone, allow `uv run python` and the two arxiv tools). They are unpaid fixes. Stage 1 results would otherwise carry a leakage path and no VERIFIED arithmetic in any arm.
5. **Stage 1 when the owner funds it: $107-140 for arms A and B, $164-213 with C** (pilot per-run cost, auditor $0.93-1.48; VERIFIED arithmetic, one draw per arm, so allow ±50 %). Per-run caps are A $2.17, B $1.26 and C $1.90 (2x stage 0). Run arms A, B and C as pre-registered, C's re-open rule unchanged. Watch C's Opus re-fetches and cache writes per trial: in the one pilot run C cost 1.5x B (§5.2), which the decision rule's $ per accepted note will price. Adopt `medium` if B's mean accuracy is within 5 points of A's; otherwise keep `high`. Do not move the researcher wholesale to Sonnet 5.5: DeepResearch Bench II puts Opus 2.4 points ahead (1.0 to 3.8), and harness-efficiency.md §3 already rejects it on citation risk.
6. **Cheaper levers do not need this experiment.** Fewer round-1 researchers per question (a median of 6 now) would save about $3-4 per run removed (SOURCED, [r2-research-and-lead-cost.md](_scratch/harness-efficiency/r2-research-and-lead-cost.md) §4, change 3). That cuts the volume half of the 50 % line without any quality change to measure.

7. **Candidate, not yet a question: `medium` first, `high` after a Blocking audit.** Independent work questions the premise that higher effort is safer. HAL ran 21,730 rollouts over 9 models and 9 benchmarks and reports "higher reasoning effort reducing accuracy in the majority of runs" ([Holistic Agent Leaderboard: The Missing Infrastructure for AI Agent Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977), Kapoor et al. 2025, arXiv, abstract). Sun et al. find that higher effort "frequently fails on tasks that lower effort solved" and that "starting at the lowest effort and escalating only after a verified failure, matches or improves on fixed top effort across all models and benchmarks at lower cost for most proprietary models" ([What Effort Level Should I Choose? On the Economics of Reasoning Effort in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491), Sun, Kwon, Hasan, Zhu, Zhou and Bianchi 2026, arXiv, abstract). Ares, a per-step effort router, "reduces reasoning token usage by up to 52.7% compared to fixed high-effort reasoning" on tasks including BrowseComp-Plus deep research ([Ares: Adaptive Reasoning Effort Selection for Efficient LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07915), Yang et al. 2026, arXiv, abstract). For this harness the analogue is the builder's escalation policy ([harness-efficiency.md](harness-efficiency.md) §3 table): run the researcher at `medium` and re-dispatch at `high` only when the research-auditor returns a Blocking finding. The research-auditor is the "verified failure". SOURCED, abstracts only, none on Opus 5.5 or on cited research notes; it is a fourth arm worth its own pre-registered question, not a change to this one (BELIEVED).
