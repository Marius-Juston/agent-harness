## 0. Findings in one paragraph
Agents file follow-ups at scale, and the problem is less "small fixes deferred" than "follow-ups that are never done and never explained". Over 2026-10-03 to 2026-10-13, agents (Opus 5.5 in 95% of subagent model fields) filed 640 items while working on another issue. 75% are still open. The ones that closed did so within 1.4 days (median 3.1 h), and their closing changes were ordinary tickets (median 1,072 net lines), not small fixes: only 4 of 143 (3%) were 30 lines or fewer. Reading 147 follow-ups in H, repo-E and repo-R by hand, about 12% look small, but most of those were blocked by a rule (another worker owned the file, a research-only role, a harness file or an owner decision). Only about 3% were small and free of any rule, the clearest being an H review finding in the PR's own new code that was filed four minutes before the PR merged. 62% of follow-ups give no reason for deferring. The cause is structural: every role has a "file it" path and a hand-back slot that asks for issue numbers, and only the tester has a "fix it" path. Anthropic's own scope guidance ("mention it at the end instead of doing it") turns into a filed issue inside a harness. The literature gives no support for "later is cheaper" (Menzies et al. 2016 found no delayed-issue effect in 171 projects). Debt is mostly repaid by the person who created it, and soon, but an agent worker exits at hand-back, so every follow-up takes the slow path of a fresh worker (42 tool calls before its first edit). **Recommendation:** one fix-now rule stated once in `multi-agent.md` §3 and linked from every role: fix in this PR when the change is at most 20 lines in at most 2 files, in the PR's diff or Owns or exposed by it, mechanical, and in a file no one else holds; a defect the PR introduced is always fixed. Add a triage step before `dev integrate` that sends fix-nows back to the worker, and require every filed follow-up to name the condition that failed. The thresholds are ASSUMED. Re-measure in 2 to 4 weeks with the same script.

## 1. Question and scope
Why do agents in a harness-run repository file a follow-up issue for work they could finish in the same change, and what rule makes them fix it now without breaking scope discipline ([QUESTION.md](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/QUESTION.md))? The scope covers how often it happens, what the deferred work cost, which instructions cause it, the literature and prior art, and a rule with thresholds. Out of scope: changing the instructions themselves (#154).

## 2. How often agents defer, and what it cost (VERIFIED)
All numbers in this section come from [measure_deferral.py](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/scripts/measure_deferral.py), unless marked otherwise (stdlib, read-only `gh`, outputs in gitignored `.scratch/`). It reads this machine's Claude Code transcripts (327 main and 659 subagent files, 2026-10-03 to 2026-10-13) and the boards and PRs of the four repositories. A follow-up is an item created by a `board new`, `gh issue create` or `gh api` call during work on another, known issue. Method details: [r1-measured](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/r1-measured.md).

- **Volume.** VERIFIED: 640 follow-ups (repo-L 493, repo-E 75, H 46, repo-R 26). Subagents filed 600 of them: builders and testers 399, researchers 172, the architect 8, other subagents 21. The lead filed 40. In one H session on 2026-10-13, 25 were filed during other work, and 21 of those were still open when measured. The models were Opus 5.5 for 94.9% of the subagent model fields in the window, and Sonnet 5.5 for 5.0% ([models_in_window.py](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/scripts/models_in_window.py)). These results describe these models only; older-model results are not comparable.
- **Done the same day or not at all.** VERIFIED: 481 of 640 (75%) are open. The closed ones merged their closing change a median of 3.1 h after filing (IQR 1.2 to 10.8 h, max 32.4 h). Of the follow-ups at least 7 days old (all repo-L), 126 of 493 (26%) closed within 7 days and 366 (74%) are still open. repo-L's sessions stopped on 2026-10-05, so "open" there means work stopped, not that the items were rejected. The H, repo-E and repo-R items are under 3.5 days old (right-censored).
- **The closed ones were tickets, not small fixes.** VERIFIED: closing diffs had a median of 1,072 net lines (IQR 551 to 1,665). Net leaves out generated docs, locks, handoffs, logs, board files and research scratch. 4 of 143 (3%) were at most 30 lines, and 10 of 143 (7%) at most 100. 67 closing changes closed several items, so per-item sizes there are upper bounds.

- **The four small closed ones were all blocked by a rule.** VERIFIED (PR files). H [#136](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/issues/136) (xfail_strict and a floor raise, 25 net lines) was filed by a researcher, whose Owns were docs only. repo-E #73 (5 lines) was a permission-settings edit, "harness file, owner decision". repo-E #182 was a 2-line wording fix found by a research audit, and repo-E #175 (20 lines) was filed by the lead from a research round.
- **Small but not blocked: about 3%.** BELIEVED (147 items in H, repo-E and repo-R read by hand; size estimated, not measured): about 17 of 147 (12%) are 30 lines or fewer, and about 4 or 5 of 147 (3%) are also free of a parallel-ownership, role, harness-file or owner-decision rule. In H that is 3 or 4 of 46. Examples: [#145](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/issues/145) is a reviewer finding in PR #142's own new allowlist (`git -c core.fsmonitor=<cmd>` runs a command). It was filed at 02:39Z, and the PR merged at 02:43Z. [#47](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/issues/47) is a constant in a file the origin PR touched, filed 2 minutes before that PR merged. In repo-R, #163 is a crash in `simulate` that the origin PR's new guard causes. Most of the rest were deferred correctly: [#146](https://github.com/Marius-Juston/agent-harness/issues/146) waited because "#114 avoided editing those lines while #142 is open".
- **The fix could usually still go in the open PR.** VERIFIED: 532 of 618 (86%) were filed before the origin PR merged. The closing change touched the origin PR's code or config files in 92 of 142 cases (65%), and its Owns in 75 of 142 (53%). repo-L's broad lanes inflate both.
- **Most give no reason.** VERIFIED (crude regex, multi-label): 395 of 640 (62%) state no reason in the filing text or the two messages before it. Where a reason shows: research gap or protocol follow-up 19%, owner decision 9%, size or "later" 7%, parallel work or hotspot 7%, found incidentally 3%, scope or Owns 2%. Verbatim reasons are in [r1-measured](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/r1-measured.md) F7.

The owner perceives frequent small deferrals. The data puts small, unblocked deferrals at about 3%, and the larger problem elsewhere: three quarters of follow-ups are never done, and most give no reason. BELIEVED: both views lead to the same fix, a named fix-now path plus a triage step that makes every deferral state its reason.

## 3. Why: the instructions that push filing (SOURCED, local files read 2026-10-12/13)
Details and line numbers: [r1-instruction-causes](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/r1-instruction-causes.md) F1.
- **Every role can file; only the tester can fix.** [tester.md](../../plugins/harness-ops/agents/tester.md) is the only fix-now rule in the surface: "A small defect inside the issue's Owns: fix it". [builder.md](../../plugins/harness-ops/agents/builder.md) allows an edit outside Owns "only when the issue needs it end to end". By that wording, an incidental find never qualifies. The builder's PR body and hand-back ask for "follow-ups filed as issues" and "follow-up issue numbers", and no role has a "fixed in passing" slot. [researcher.md](../../plugins/harness-ops/agents/researcher.md) says "File each important gap you did not close as an issue", and round mode forbids edits. The architect files "one issue per step".
- **The channel.** BELIEVED: "Work without an issue does not exist" in [HARNESS.md](../../template/.devkit/HARNESS.md.jinja), together with Owns, hotspots belonging to the lead ([multi-agent.md](../specs/multi-agent.md) §3), "the lead never builds" ([lead skill](../../plugins/harness-ops/skills/lead/SKILL.md)) and "one PR per issue", leaves a new issue as the only legal move for a fix found in passing. No rule is wrong on its own. The gap is that none of them names the cheap case.
- **Gates.** VERIFIED: before #150, the weaken check flagged turning `xfail_strict` on as a weakening, so H #136 could not have been a fix-now. At tip `da5575a`, [xfail_strict_probe.py](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/scripts/xfail_strict_probe.py) returns no finding, so this is fixed. A floor raise passes without approval, while a lowered floor is refused ([floor_raise_probe.py](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/scripts/floor_raise_probe.py)). The gate only prints the raise as a note, and `.devkit/ratchets.toml` is in no ordinary issue's Owns.
- **The model layer.** SOURCED: Anthropic's [Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5) says the model "can also expand the scope of a task". For narrow tasks it suggests saying so "in a sentence and continue with the task as asked rather than quietly narrowing, widening, or transforming it". [Prompting Claude Sonnet 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-sonnet-5-5) suggests: "If you think one would help, mention it at the end instead of doing it". [Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1) says to "report it as a follow-up in your summary". [Prompting Claude Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5) calls a list of non-blocking decisions at the end of a turn unwanted. BELIEVED: in a harness, "mention it" becomes `board new`. The protection against scope creep is real, so the fix should be a narrow, named exception, not a looser scope rule.

## 4. What the literature and other harnesses say
Details: [r1-literature](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/r1-literature.md).
- **Deferred debt is repaid by its author, soon, or very late.** SOURCED: most self-admitted technical debt is removed by the person who introduced it ([An Empirical Study on the Removal of Self-Admitted Technical Debt](https://doi.org/10.1109/icsme.2017.8), Maldonado et al., ICSME 2017). Median repayment of 152 debt items in two Apache projects was 25.0 h, against a mean of 872.3 h ([Identification and Remediation of Self-Admitted Technical Debt in Issue Trackers](https://doi.org/10.1109/seaa51224.2020.00083), Li, Soliman and Avgeriou, SEAA 2020). VERIFIED with [breakeven.py](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/scripts/breakeven.py): the mean is 34.9 times the median. 20 to 50% of debt "removals" are accidental, so comment-based survival overstates repayment ([Was self-admitted technical debt removal a real removal?](https://doi.org/10.1145/3196398.3196423), Zampetti et al., MSR 2018). BELIEVED: the fast repayer is "the same person, soon", which an exited agent worker cannot be.
- **Later is not shown to cost more.** SOURCED: Menzies et al. (171 projects) found no delayed-issue effect ([Are Delayed Issues Harder to Resolve?](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04886), EMSE 2016). So the case for fixing now rests on the agent's fixed per-item overhead, not on escalating defect cost. The overhead is 42 tool calls before the first edit and 138 per builder run (median, [agent-harness.md](agent-harness.md) §6), plus an issue, a PR, a gate and an integration.
- **The break-even point.** VERIFIED arithmetic with ASSUMED inputs ([breakeven.py](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/scripts/breakeven.py)). Fixing now costs f(1+ρ), where ρ is the added review and tangling cost. Deferring costs F + p(f+O): F = 3 calls to file, O = 42 calls of overhead, p = the probability the follow-up is ever done, and no value is lost if it never is (the case most favourable to deferring). At the measured p = 0.26, fixing now wins below 19, 14 and 11 tool calls for ρ = 0, 0.25 and 0.5. At p = 1 it wins below 90 calls, or always when ρ = 0. The more likely a follow-up is to be done, the stronger the case for doing it now.
- **Tangling: a bound, not a veto.** SOURCED: up to 15% of bug fixes are tangled ([The impact of tangled code changes](https://doi.org/10.1109/msr.2013.6624018), Herzig and Zeller, MSR 2013). In a controlled experiment (N = 28), decomposed changes reduced wrongly reported issues but did not change the defects found or the understanding of the change ([The effects of change decomposition on code review](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj-cs.193), Di Biase et al., PeerJ CS 2019). Google's guide keeps refactorings in their own change, but "small cleanups such as fixing a local variable name can be included". About 100 lines is "usually a reasonable size" ([Small CLs](https://github.com/google/eng-practices/blob/master/review/developer/small-cls.md), Google eng-practices). Of 567 Claude Code PRs, about 40% combined tasks. 83.8% were merged, against 91.0% of matched human PRs, and only 3.3% of the rejections cited size ([On the Use of Agentic Coding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14745), Watanabe et al., TOSEM 2025).
- **Boy-scout rule: recommended, but no outcome data.** SOURCED: Fowler says to leave code better than you found it, with green tests, and to "come back the same day" for what you note ([Opportunistic Refactoring](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/OpportunisticRefactoring.html), Fowler 2011). Some refactoring types induce faults ([When Does a Refactoring Induce Bugs?](https://doi.org/10.1109/scam.2012.20), Bavota et al., SCAM 2012). So the rule covers small, test-covered fixes, not structural refactors.
- **Closing the loop is the prior art that matters.** SOURCED: Google says that unless clean-up happens "immediately after the present CL, it never happens". Complexity a change introduces "must be cleaned up before submission". A problem the change only exposes goes in a bug the author assigns to themselves ([Handling pushback](https://github.com/google/eng-practices/blob/master/review/reviewer/pushback.md), Google eng-practices). [obra/superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers) (MIT) keeps deferred minors in a ledger that the final review triages before merge: "A roll-up nobody reads is a silent discard." [openai/codex](https://github.com/openai/codex), [SWE-agent](https://github.com/SWE-agent/SWE-agent) and [OpenHands](https://github.com/OpenHands/software-agent-sdk) say "minimal changes, mention the rest", and none of them closes the loop. None of these sets a numeric threshold.

## 5. The fix-now rule
BELIEVED for the rule, ASSUMED for the numbers. **Fix it in this PR, listed under `Fixed in passing` in the PR body, when all four hold:**
1. **Small.** At most 20 changed lines in at most 2 files.
2. **Here.** The files are in the PR's diff or Owns, or this change exposed the defect.
3. **Mechanical.** It needs no design choice, new interface or dependency, or owner approval, and the gate checks it.
4. **Free.** No other open item or hotspot holds the file.

A defect the PR itself introduced is always fixed, whatever its size (Google's rule). Anything else is filed with the condition that failed as its reason. Roles that cannot edit (a researcher, the architect) batch fix-ready edits (file, change, check) into one build issue instead of one issue each.

The bounds and their basis:
- 20 lines and 2 files sit far below Google's 100-line norm, and in the range of its "local variable name" cleanup. ASSUMED: a 20-line edit in two files that are already open takes fewer tool calls than the 11 to 19 break-even at the measured p.
- The rule leaves the four protections intact: disjoint Owns for parallel workers (4), evidence before design (3), the weaken and ratchet gates (3), and one record per change (the PR body).
- It would have changed about 3% of follow-ups by count, and #145-type review findings by kind. The larger effect expected is on the 62% that state no reason, through the triage step.

## 7. Limits and how to check the rule
- Right-censoring: the H, repo-E and repo-R follow-ups are under 4 days old, and the small-size share (12%, 3%) comes from one rater's reading. Treat both as orders of magnitude. VERIFIED counts cover one machine and 10 days of transcripts.
- The thresholds (20 lines, 2 files) and the break-even inputs (F, ρ) are ASSUMED. Only 4 small closed follow-ups exist, too few to fit a threshold.
- No controlled test shows that the wording changes behaviour. The re-measurement below is observational. A `dev eval` scenario with a planted two-line adjacent defect would test it directly.
- The Claude Code system prompt's scope lines were checked only in a third-party capture dated 2025-12-09 ([r1-instruction-causes](_scratch/fix-now-vs-defer/r1-instruction-causes.md) F4).
